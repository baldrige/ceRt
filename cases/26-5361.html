Donna Huff v. Los Angeles County Sheriff's Department, et al.

IFP petition · United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, No. 25-3772 · judgment September 4, 2025

Pending

Question presented

This case addresses critical errors by the lower courts regarding jurisdictional deadlines and the civil rights of disabled pro se litigants. The District Court and Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals rigidly dismissed Petitioner's appeal for being exactly "one day late," concluding under 28 U.S.C. § 2107 and Bowles v. Russell, 551 U.S. 205 (2007) that they lacked authority to grant equitable exceptions. This holding directly conflicts with Boechler, P.C. v. Commissioner, 596 U.S. 83 (2022), where this Court unanimously held that such filing deadlines are non-jurisdictional claim-processing rules open to equitable tolling under extraordinary circumstances. Petitioner, a disabled pro se individual recovering from extensive reconstructive surgery on their left hand, was physically present at the courthouse on the final filing date with a 500-page document. The court clerk reviewed the filing for over thirty minutes but instructed Petitioner to return "tomorrow" due to a counting preference. Despite Petitioner warning that the filing would then be late, the clerk walked away. Returning the next morning as ordered, Petitioner was forced to file one day late. The lower courts' refusal to recognize this clerk-directed impediment as grounds for equitable tolling represents a severe departure from standard judicial procedures. The underlying civil rights actions arise under 42 U.S.C. § 1983 and the Americans with Disabilities Act. Sheriffs deputies subjected Petitioner to brutal excessive force by applying handcuffs in a manner designed to cause pain, ignoring explicit warnings regarding a lifelong congenital physical disability affecting die arms, wrists, and shoulders. The constraints were so tight they severed the tendons holding Petitioner's thumb and severely tore the wrists, elbows, and shoulder. Petitioner was then left in the back of a police cruiser for three and a half hours without medical accommodations or adjustment No compensation has been provided for these catastrophic and permanent injuries.
