Questions presented

In B&B Hardware, Inc. v. Hargis Industries, Inc., 575 U.S. 138 (2015), this Court established a uniform rule for the identity-of-issue element of federal trademark preclusion. It held categorically that the Trademark Trial and Appeal Board (TTAB) and federal district courts apply the same likelihood-ofconfusion standard for registration and infringement under the Lanham Act. Id. at 154. In doing so, this Court reversed a judgment that had expressly adopted the Second Circuit’s rule that differing multifactor analyses render the two standards distinct. See 716 F.3d 1020, 1024-1025 (8th Cir. 2013) (adopting Jim Beam Brands Co. v. Beamish & Crawford, Ltd., 937 F.2d 729 (2d Cir. 1991), and Levy v. Kosher Overseers Ass’n of America, Inc., 104 F.3d 38 (2d Cir. 1997)). Having settled the legal standard, B&B Hardware made identity purely factual: “[i]f a mark owner uses its mark in ways that are materially the same as the usages included in its registration application, then the TTAB is deciding the same likelihood-of-confusion issue as a district court in infringement litigation.” 575 U.S. at 156. Applying this test to nearly identical marks (LIANA and LIANO) on identical goods (wine), the district court found the issue identical, the other elements met, and held Peju precluded. The Second Circuit vacated on both fronts. It revived its abrogated precedents, Jim Beam and Levy, and sua sponte created a new rule for factual identity making preclusion under B&B Hardware impossible. The questions presented are:

  1. Whether the Second Circuit’s pre-B&B Hardware rule conditioning issue preclusion on a case-by-case comparison of how tribunals apply their respective multifactor tests conflicts with B&B Hardware’s categorical holding that the likelihood-ofconfusion standard for registration and infringement under the Lanham Act is the same.

  2. Whether the Second Circuit’s new rule—that a marketplace use “narrower” than an unrestricted registration application defeats identity of the issue— conflicts with B&B Hardware’s “included-usages” test.
