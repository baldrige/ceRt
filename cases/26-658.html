Questions presented

The Family Smoking Prevention and Tobacco Control Act (TCA) authorizes the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) to adopt “tobacco product standards” which govern the “construction, components, ingredients, additives, constituents, ... and properties” of tobacco products. 21 U.S.C. §§ 387g(a)(3)–(4). The TCA requires APA notice-andcomment rulemaking. ID. at § 387g(c). FDA must deny a premarket tobacco product application (PMTA) for products that do not conform to these standards unless there is “adequate information to justify the deviation” therefrom. Id. § 387j(c)(2)(D). Without rulemaking, FDA has required every non-tobacco-flavored product to statistically outperform a tobacco-flavored product in converting smokers. The court below held this requirement is not a product standard, but “a balancing test, not a ban.” Separately, regulating tobacco is a major question. FDA v. Brown & Williamson Tobacco Corp., 529 U.S. 120, 159–60 (2000). The TCA immediately subjected four cigarette-related product categories to FDA regulation but left it to decide which “other tobacco products” to regulate through a regulatory deeming. 21 U.S.C. § 387a(b). The questions presented are:

  1. Whether FDA’s uniform condition on the marketing of every non-tobacco-flavored vaping product—enforcement of a flavortriggered comparative efficacy requirement— is a “tobacco product standard” for which the TCA § 387j required notice-and-comment - ii - rulemaking. This question parallels the one this Court left open in FDA v. Wages and White Lion Investments, L.L.C., 604 U.S. 542, 565 (2025).

  2. Whether § 387a(b)’s “deeming” provision is unconstitutional because Article I, enforced through the major questions doctrine, required Congress to specifically decide which “other tobacco products” to regulate.
