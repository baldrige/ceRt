Question presented

The Federal Tort Claims Act (FTCA), 28 U.S.C. §§ 1346(b), 2671 et seq., waives the sovereign immunity of the United States, subject to thirteen enumerated exceptions. Under one of those exceptions, the United States remains immune to “[a]ny claim arising in respect of the assessment or collection of any tax or customs duty, or the detention of any goods, merchandise, or other property by any officer of customs or excise or any other law enforcement officer.” 28 U.S.C. § 2680(c). Congress amended the FTCA in 1974 to provide a remedy for victims of deliberate misconduct by federal law-enforcement officers. This “law enforcement proviso” extends the waiver of sovereign immunity to “any claim … arising out of assault, battery, false imprisonment, false arrest, abuse of process, or malicious prosecution” committed by federal law enforcement officers. 28 U.S.C. § 2680(h). The Fifth Circuit held—in conflict with the Ninth Circuit—that the customs-duty exception in § 2680(c) bars intentional-tort claims against Border Patrol officers, otherwise viable under § 2680(h), because the torts occurred while Petitioner’s personal property was also detained. The question presented is: Whether § 2680(c) immunizes the United States from intentional tort claims that fall squarely within § 2680(h)’s waiver of sovereign immunity, whenever such torts happen to coincide with detention of the victim’s personal property.
