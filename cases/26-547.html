Questions presented

The Sixth Amendment guarantees the criminally accused a trial by an impartial jury, and this Court held in McDonough Power Equip., Inc. v. Greenwood, 464 U.S. 548, 556 (1984), that a new trial is required where a juror “failed to answer honestly a material question on voir dire” and “a correct response would have provided a valid basis for a challenge for cause.” The federal courts of appeals and the state courts of last resort are openly divided over how that standard governs juror concealment: whether relief turns on proof that the juror’s false answer was deliberate, and whether a defendant may obtain relief by showing implied or actual bias independent of the juror’s honesty. Here, a seated juror gave a false answer concealing that she had been a complainant, witness, and victim in a criminal matter and that her son and former husband had been accused in criminal cases, and the trial court denied a new trial solely because it could not find that the false answer was intentional. The questions presented are:

  1. Whether the Sixth Amendment right to an impartial jury is denied – and a new trial required – when a seated juror gives a false answer during voir dire concealing that she and members of her family were accused, complainants, witnesses, or victims in criminal cases, and whether a defendant’s entitlement to relief turns on proof that the juror’s false answer was deliberate or on the objective effect of the concealment on the defendant’s right to an impartial jury.

  2. Whether a defendant charged with a serious felony is deprived of his right, under the Sixth and Fourteenth Amendments, to a trial by a twelve-person jury – the question on which this Court granted certiorari in Kian v. Florida, No. 25-6623 – and whether, because the Petitioner’s conviction remains pending on direct review, this petition should be held pending the decision in Kian and disposed of accordingly.
