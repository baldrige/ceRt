Craig Malin v. Sarah Watson, et al.

Question presented

In 2022, a court informed Respondent Lee Enterprises that the "truth or falsity" of its 2015 casinorelocation reporting referencing Petitioner Craig Malin was "not definitively settled." Instead of correcting its reporting to comport with documents it then possessed, in 2023 Lee republished its unsettled casino controversy narrative to new audiences, again naming Malin. While doing so, Lee was using undisclosed technology to redirect exculpatory facts away from its newsrooms. Malin asked Iowa courts whether Lee’s redirection of facts away from newsrooms created a jury question of purposeful avoidance of truth under Harte-Hanks Communications, Inc. v. Connaughton, 491 U.S. 657 (1989). Iowa courts refused to answer. Instead, they imposed Sullivan's actual-malice burden by declaring Malin a public figure while refusing to decide whether Lee’s conduct crossed the actual-malice boundary. On appeal, the constitutional question was deemed “not preserved” because the district court did not rule on it. This case does not challenge New York Times Co. v. Sullivan, 376 U.S. 254 (1964). It asks this Court to enforce actual-malice boundaries in the digital age: who is a public figure, can a media corporation use technology to secretly blind its own journalists to truth, and can fact become “opinion”? The questions presented are: I. Whether an appellate court conducting independent review under Bose Corp. v. Consumers Union, 466 U.S. 485 (1984), may classify a private citizen as a public figure under Gertz v. Robert Welch, Inc., 418 U.S. 323 (1974), based on (1) being an affiant in civil litigation, (2) information attributed to Petitioner's website without evidence it was there at publication, or (3) postpublication communications to the publisher.
