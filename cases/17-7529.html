Carlos Barbosa v. United States

IFP petition · United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit, No. 16-41575 · judgment October 2, 2017

Certiorari denied · February 20, 2018

Question presented

Petitioner, CARLOS BARBOSA, was charged with and pleaded guilty to conspiring to transport undocumented aliens within the United States. The District Court imposed a Guidelines-range sentence of 12 months and 1 day. On direct appeal, Mr. Barbosa argued he should have received a minor or mitigating role downward adjustment under the Guidelines. Mr. Barbosa argued review was for plain error. The Government responded that this claim was not reviewable on appeal because it is a fact question.. Alternatively, the Government contended a “shock the conscience” standard for finding plain error is applicable. The Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals determined that Mr. Barbosa did not have to shock the conscience to show plain error. The Appellate Court also determined, without explanation, that Mr. Barbosa had not shown plain error. The ruling of the Fifth Circuit affirmed the Government’s position that there was to be “no review” of this claim as the Government had advocated. With respect to any factual findings by the District Court, those facts must not change when the issues change, contrary to the Government’s advocacy to the contrary. Finally, the action by the Fifth Circuit reflects the Fifth Circuit did not apply the plain error standard of review, as required by this Court. Hence, the Fifth Circuit has decided an important federal question in a way that conflicts with relevant decisions of this Court. A compelling reason is thus presented in support of discretionary review by this Honorable Court. Mr. Barbosa therefore respectfully requests that this Honorable Court grant this Petition for Writ of Certiorari and allow this case to proceed to resentencing with a reduction for minor/minimal party status.
