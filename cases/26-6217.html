In Re Jamie Blunder

IFP petition

Pending

Questions presented

  1. Why did the Court of Appeals fail to authorize my timely 2255 H-1 (Appeal 24-164 Doc 2-2) petition for “Newly Discovered Evidence” of the Charlotte Mecklenburg Police Department(CMPD) K-9 activity report and Charlotte Mecklenburg Police Department (CMPD)-Canine Unit standard operating procedure (SOP) proving misrepresentation, fabrication, and concealment of material facts by the government in violations of Brady v. Maryland, 373 U.S. 83, 83 S. Ct. 1194 (1963) when all Gate­ keeping-requirements had been met?

  2. Why did the District Court and Appellate Court construe my timely pro’se second-in-time 2255 petition as a second and successive 2255, when the FOIA request/reply dates and time lines clearly demonstrates the claim of the fabricated CMPD positive K-9 Alert, couldn’t be raised in the initial 2255 application due to deliberate concealment and impediment by the government? Petitioner attests to the importance of these questions in regards to how to properly construe newly discovered evidence claims - as second-in-time or second and successive -- it presents a circuit split on the proper avenue of defendant’s claims of flagrant governmental misconduct. (See Scott v. United States, 890 F.3d 1239, 1253-54 {11th Cir. 2018})(Finding it was constrained to its holding, but imploring the Court to revisit precedent because it was wrongly decided). (See also Bernard v. United States, 141 S. Ct. 504 {2020})(Sotomayor, dissenting)(Explaining that a recently found Brady claim raised in a second-in-time 28 U.S.C 2255 petition should not be considered second or successive because (“The Fifth Circuit got it wrong. Its illogical rule conflicts with this Court’s precedent, and it rewards prosecutors who successfully conceal their Brady and Napue violations until after an inmate has sought relief from his convictions on other grounds.”) The underlying questions presented are extremely important. In sum, it asks the vital question of what is considered the higher authority, The United States Constitution, binding precedent U.S. Supreme Court case law or procedure. It is especially important to pro’se individuals who are continually thwarted, frustrated, impeded, or barred by procedure to protect governmental misconduct, in contradiction to the accordance of fundamental fairness and the guaranteed protections by the United States Constitution, which is the Supreme Law of the Land, and ensures Due process for all U.S. citizens.
