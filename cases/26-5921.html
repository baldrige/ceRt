Elhadj Alpha Mahmoud Souare v. Ohio Bureau of Motor Vehicles, et al.

IFP petition · United States Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit, No. 25-3603 · judgment March 11, 2026

Pending

Questions presented

  1. Whether the Sixth Circuit’s decision upholding Ohio’s automatic driver's license suspension scheme under R.C. §§ 3123.55(A), 3123.58, and 3123.60—which mandates suspension without pre-deprivation notice or hearing and expressly prohibits the state agency from granting any appeal—conflicts with this Court's holding in Bell v. Burson, 402 U.S. 535 (1971), and deepens an explicit Circuit split with the Fourth Circuit's en banc decision in

  2. Stinnie v. Holcomb, 55 F.4th 951 (4th Cir. 2022).

  3. Whether the lower courts erred in dismissing Petitioner's claims WITH PREJUDICE on procedural and immunity grounds, where the dismissal permanently forecloses all judicial remedies under principles of res judicata and claim preclusion, despite written, sworn admissions by state respondents that the underlying suspension was factually baseless and executed without due process.

  4. Whether a state motor vehicle agency is immune from suit under the Eleventh Amendment when enforcing a concededly unconstitutional statutory scheme, where the agency's own sworn admissions establish that it suspends licenses prior to notice through a purely mechanical, nondiscretionary act.

  5. Whether a district court's blanket, non-individualized certification under 28 U.S.C. § 1915(a)(3) across multiple actions by an indigent pro se litigant conflicts with Coppedge v. United States, 369 U.S. 438 (1962), and violates Griffin v. Illinois, 351 U.S. 12 (1956), and M.L.B. v. S.L.J., 519 U.S. 102 (1996), by creating an impenetrable procedural barrier to appellate review.

  6. Whether a state agency's repeated imposition of license suspensions following written admissions of error and non­ responses to statutory hearing requests constitutes a pattern of deliberate constitutional defiance warranting this Court’s supervisory intervention.
