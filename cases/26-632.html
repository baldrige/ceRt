Frank William Bonan, II, Individually and as an Institution-Affiliated Party of Grand Rivers Community Bank, Grand Chain, Illinois (Insured State Nonmember Bank) v. Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation

Questions presented

This case presents questions under the Seventh Amendment and Article III that the court below described as “close and challenging” and that, it predicted, “[i]n time the Justices are sure to consider.” Pet.App. 18a, 21a. Following administrative proceedings conducted before an Administrative Law Judge, the Board of Directors of the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (“FDIC”) found that Petitioner had breached fiduciary duties owed to a state-chartered, FDIC-insured bank. On that basis, it barred Petitioner from working at federally insured banks and imposed a $105,000 civil penalty. The Seventh Circuit upheld both sanctions, concluding that while the claims before the FDIC “implicated the Seventh Amendment,” Pet.App. 8a, Congress could entrust them to an administrative agency —and thereby avoid a jury trial —under the socalled “public rights exception” to Article III. In doing so, the court acknowledged that by the time Congress authorized the FDIC to bring breach-of-fiduciary-duty claims in administrative proceedings in 1966, such claims had acquired “common law soil.” Pet.App. 17a. But it regarded that development as not “legally significant” and, characterizing fiduciary-duty claims as equitable at the Founding, held that they involved public rights. Pet.App. 18a. The questions presented are:

  1. Whether a statutory claim that seeks classic legal relief, thus implicating the Seventh Amendment jurytrial right, and effectively codifies a recognized commonlaw claim may be assigned to an administrative agency for resolution without a jury if, as the court below concluded, the common-law claim had not been explicitly recognized at the Founding.

  2. Whether the “public rights exception” permits Congress to assign statutory claims seeking classic legal relief, thus implicating the Seventh Amendment jury-trial right, to an administrative agency for resolution without a jury if similar claims were considered by courts in equity in 1789.
