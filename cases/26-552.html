Questions presented

Congress enacted 26 U.S.C. § 7623 to ensure that whistleblowers who provide specific information leading to the collection of unpaid taxes receive mandatory awards. Section 7623(c) defines the scope of “collected proceeds” and expands award eligibility, including proceeds paid by related taxpayers and public companies. The courts below upheld the denial of a whistleblower award by adopting extra-textual limitations that Congress did not enact. The questions presented are:

  1. Whether courts may narrow the mandatory whistleblower award provisions of 26 U.S.C. § 7623(b) by disregarding § 7623(c), contrary to the statutory text and Congress’s directive that qualifying whistleblowers “shall” receive awards.

  2. Whether “collected proceeds” under 26 U.S.C. § 7623(b) and § 7623(c) include tax, penalty, and interest amounts disclosed in SEC Forms 10-Q and 10-K by a public company when those payments result from Internal Revenue Service (IRS) enforcement actions triggered by whistleblower information.

  3. Whether the court of appeals erred by deferring to the IRS without conducting a complete statutory analysis or requiring reasoned decision-making, thereby insulating agency action from meaningful judicial review. -I- Page of -X -II-
