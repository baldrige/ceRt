Michael W. Lister v. Tom McGinley, Superintendent, State Correctional Institution at Coal Township, et al.

IFP petition · United States Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit, No. 17-3609 · judgment March 13, 2018

Certiorari denied · October 1, 2018

Question presented

SUMMARY Mr. Lister alleged that the issues of his trial counsel raised some debate among reasonable jurors. The record before the state court shows that the prosecutor tried to impeach Dr. Cooke, a defense witness certified as an expert in psychology, by surreptitiously exploiting a gap in the jury's knowledge about matters relating to the presentation of expert testimony. In particular, the prosecutor asked Dr. Cooke on the witness stand to express his personal "opinion" as opposed to his professional opinion about Mr. Lister's perception with respect to his mistaken belief that deadly force was required to protect himself against the treat of death or serious - bodily injury. When Doctor Cooke refused to express his personal opinion, leaving that for the jury to determine (N.T. 10/8/09, at 65), a cloud of suspicion was cast over the testimony provided by this critical defense witness in which it raised the possibility of a debate among reasonable jurors through the prosecutor's questioning in violation of Mr. Lister's due process at trial. QUESTION ONE: Should the appeals court remand toacircuit court for a hearing, when there is a dispute between a prosecutor's personal intention On a expert's opinion;'when defense attorney fails to engage to support his own expert's findings by objecting to a DA's prejudice in front of the jury? UESTION TWO: Did the appeals court of the Third Circuit abuse its discretion when it accepted the district court's denial of Petitioner's C.O.A. without remanding these proceedings for a hearing to settle the dispute of an expert's findings and a prosecutor's personal feelings among the jury? QUESTION THREE: Should a defense attorney object when a prosecutor has stepped outside of professional responsibility by committing a personal attack on an expert witness and defense by implying to a jury that their defense is only about money, thereby crossing ethical lines to get a conviction?
