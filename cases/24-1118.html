Questions presented

  1. Under 28 U.S.C. § 455(a), federal judges are statutorily disqualified from hearing cases whenever their "impartiality might reasonably be questioned." The Supreme Court held that disqualification was required as a matter of constitutional due process in multiple precedents. Why could Connecticut federal district judge Victor A. Bolden illegally and unconstitutionally deny recusal in violation of Supreme Court precedent? 2. Whether the circuit court should recognize the interlocutory appeal as a “substantial legal control issue” that prevents a statutorily and constitutionally unqualified judge from continuing to hear the case, and exercise jurisdiction to grant an immediate interim review, when an aggrieved party challenges a judge’s refusal to disqualify? or Whether an appeal by an aggrieved party challenges a judge's refusal to disqualify should be automatically defined as a complaint alleging a federal judge has committed misconduct or has a disability that interferes with the performance of their judicial duties, and processed under The Judicial Conduct and Disability Act of 1980(“Act’), 28 U.S.C. § 351-364, and the Rules for Judicial- Conduct and Judicial- Disability Proceedings(“Rule”), as amended on March 12,2019. l U-
