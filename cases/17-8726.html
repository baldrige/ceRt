Perry Turner v. City of Los Angeles, California, et al.

IFP petition · Court of Appeal of California, Second Appellate District, No. B262554 · judgment September 11, 2017

Certiorari denied · May 29, 2018

Question presented

The questions presented for review speak to a right, privilege, or immunity specially set up or claimed under the Constitution of the United States (28 U.S. Code § 1257), framed concisely in relation to the case (Rule 14), are these: Should privacy protections in the United States and California Constitutions be interpreted as meaning in part that there is and should be a Constitutionally dimensioned privacy right not to be expected by law to worry, or to have worried, as though paranoid about unknown and practicably unimaginable events during delayed discovery and during fraudulent concealment? This arose in context of delayed discovery and fraudulent concealment rules. Since statutory tort claims procedures anticipate an antecedent claim, and if need be also application for leave to file late claim and if that is denied then also petition to the Superior Court for relief from requirement to file a claim, before facts can be properly alleged in verified claim or complaint while their discovery is delayed concurrently by consequences of alleged tortfeasors’ own acts and their own fraudulent concealments of strict accrual, and since Appellate Districts differ over whether accrual dates, discovery delayed by consequences of tort, fraudulent concealment of accruals, claim filing dates, filing claims, not filing claims, filing claims late, denials of leave by entities, or in virtual vacuo only “mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect” fall within trial courts’ purview, exactly what issues are or are not properly before a trial court in hearing a petition for relief when a claim is actually filed but whether it is even late is in dispute before those facts can be properly even alleged in a verified claim or verified pleading, alike? III. Opinions are split among Divisions of the Second District Court of Appeal, as well as among Districts of the Court of Appeal, in applying principles elucidated by the Supreme Court over metes and bounds of delayed discovery and fraudulent concealment in relation both to commonly understood lexical and judicially redefined terms for mistake, inadvertence, surprise, and excusable neglect, and to unavoidably extenuating practical and personal circumstances of forcible and violent home invasion that effectively destroys the home and leaves victims in chaos that impedes their filing tort claims.
