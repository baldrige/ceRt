Lauro Abel Grimaldo v. United States

IFP petition · United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit, No. 16-41091 · judgment August 2, 2017

Certiorari denied · October 1, 2018

Question presented

QUESTION I IN. LIGHT OF THE FLORES-FIGUEROA V UNITED STATES, 556 U.S. 646 (2009), AND ABRAMSKI V UNITED STATES, 573 U.S. , 134 S.CT. 189 L.ED 2d 62 2014 LEXIS 4170 (2014) DOES THE “KNOWINGLY OR INTENTIONALLY” MENS REA CONTAINED IN 21 U.S.C. § 841(a) APPLY TO THE OFFENSE ELEMENTS OF DRUG TYPE AND DRUG QUANTITY FOUND IN 21 U.S.C. § 841(b), AND U.S.C. § 846? Finally the importance of the issue presented here is underscored by the fact that drug type and quantity may elevate the statutory maximum for a drug trafficking offense under § 841 from one year in prison all the way up to life imprisonment. “Indeed, when viewed in terms of the potential difference in restrictions of personal liberty attendant to [the type and quantity gradations found in § 841(b)], the distinction between [those gradation] may be of greater importance than the difference between guilt or innocence for many lesser crimes.” Mullaney v Wilbur, 421 U.S. 684, 698 (1975). That being the case, the offense elements of drug type and drug quantity in § 841(b) are truly the tail wagging the dog of the “verb” elements found in § 841*a) (distributing, manufacturing, etc.), and it is perverse to assign a mens rea to the latter but not to the former. In sum, one commentator has noted: [t}he Court’s decision [in Flores--Figueroa show that lower courts should not automatically interpret any criminal statute in a broad manner, totally disregard defendant’s relative degrees of culpability, thus, the Court’s holding has the potential to bring punishment closer to the defendants’ blameworthiness. Lower courts should follow the Court’s lead in Flores-Figueroa and examine a statute’s -t language to determine the type of behavior targeted by the statute at issue to ensure that harsh minimum sentence are not applied more broadly than conduct requires. Mens Rea Requirement, 123 Har.L.Rev. 312, 322 (2009). Because, however, the lower courts remain unclear about how to apply the teachings of Flores-Figueroa to the interpretation of other criminal statutes, this Court should grant this petition in order to bring needed clarity to the field. QUESTION II IS A CONVICTION AND SENTENCE UNDER 21 U.S.C. § 841 (a)(1), AND 21 U.S.C. § 846 A VIOLATION OF FIFTH AMENDMENT NOTICE RIGHTS AND SIXTH AMENDMENT DUE PROCESS RIGHTS BECAUSE THE JURY VERDICT, CONVICTION AND SENTENCE ARE BASED ON JURY INSTRUCTIONS THAT ALLOW FOR THE PRESUMPTION OF INTENT THEREBY LESSENING THE GOVERNMENT’S BURDEN OF PROOF? 1]
