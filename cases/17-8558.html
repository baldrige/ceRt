Gillman Roddy Long v. United States

IFP petition · United States Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit, No. 16-2820 · judgment November 9, 2017

Certiorari denied · October 9, 2018

Question presented

In 2009, Petitioner Gillman Long voluntarily met with an FBI agent, at the agent’s request. The FBI agent’s purpose for the meeting was to investigate an allegation of sexual abuse against him by his stepdaughter. When Mr. Long realized the purpose of the meeting, he stated to the FBI agent, “I do not want to incriminate myself. 1 would like to stop talking.” He was subsequently charged with 3 counts of sexual abuse of a minor. At trial, Mr. Long did not testify but the prosecutor introduced Mr. Long’s statement and also argued that it provided proof of Mr. Long’s guilt. No objection was made by counsel for Mr. Long, the evidence and argument were allowed to stand, and he was found guilty on 2 of the 3 counts. On direct appeal, the Court of Appeals held that no plain error occurred because the admissibility of the statement was an open question in the Eighth Circuit in spite of the fact that other Circuits had held that such evidence was inadmissible and violative of the Fifth Amendment self incrimination clause. Mr. Long subsequently filed a motion pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 2255 alleging, inter alia, ineffective assistance of counsel due to counsel’s failure to object to the admission of the statement and argument. The district court denied the motion, holding that, since the admissibility of the statement and argument had been and still were an open question in the Eighth Circuit, counsel’s performance could not by law have been below the objective standard of Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668, 80 L.Ed.2d 674, 104 S.Ct. 2052 (1984). While the district court granted a Certificate of Appealability, the Court of Appeals affirmed the dismissal of the Section 2255 motion and claim. 1.) Does a bright line rule, that an attorney only fails the perfbrmance prong of the Strickland test for failure to raise an objection based on binding circuit or Supreme Court precedent, violate the Sixth Amendment?
