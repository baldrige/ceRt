Questions presented

The underlying state court proceedings, including appellate review, took place during the pendency of Chapter 7 Bankruptcy filing by the Respondent, Na­ thaniel Chibuzor Keita, which was concealed from the Petitioners and the state courts, resulting in the entry and enforcement of a judgment in violation of federal law.

  1. Does the Supremacy Clause permit a state court to avoid the self-executing effect of a Fed­ eral Bankruptcy Discharge by ruling that federal filings are not "automatically impugned" to state proceedings? (Appendix T-2-B, 165a).

  2. Whether a state court violates the Supremacy Clause and the "no fair ground of doubt" standard established in Taggart v. Lorenzen, 587 U.S. 554, 561 (2019), by refusing to vacate judg­ ments rendered in undisputed violation of the federal automatic stay, and permitting enforce­ ment of a judgment concealed from the bank­ ruptcy court, based on a determination that fed­ eral bankruptcy law is "inapplicable" to state court proceedings. (Appendices C, 14a; T-l-A, 154a).

  3. Whether the "objective reasonableness" standard for civil contempt established in Tag­ gart, 587 U.S. at 561, is defeated when a party commits fraud on the court by maintaining con­ tradictory evidence across jurisdictions — dis­ missed by the trial court as merely saying differ­ ent things in different courts — to enforce a judg­ ment rendered in undisputed violation of the fed­ eral automatic stay. (Appendices I, 63a; T-l-D, 157a; T-l-B, 156a; T-3, 170a).

  4. Whether a state court violates the Due Process Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment by summarily rejecting a meritorious defense of judgment voidness and denying a meaningful fo­ rum for relief against a debt predicated on a party’s intentional concealment of a bankruptcy filing. (Appendices H, 49a; M, 96a; N, 100a).
