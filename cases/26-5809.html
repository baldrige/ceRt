Abrahim Fata v. Abraham Ortiz, et al.

IFP petition · United States Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit, No. 25-1887

Pending

Questions presented

PURSUANT TO SUPREME COURT RULE 12.4, this single petition seeks review of six judgments from the same court involving identical or closely related questions:

  1. The Misjoinder Conflict (Rule 21): Whether Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 21, which mandates that "misjoinder of parties is not a ground for dismissing an action," prohibits a court from dismissing a pro se litigant’s personal claims with prejudice solely because the litigant improperly attempted to join or represent his minor children.

  2. The "Self-Adjudication" Conflict (28 U.S.C. § 144): Whether a district judge violates the mandatory "shall proceed no further" command of § 144 by personally adjudicating the sufficiency of a bias affidavit against himself, rather than referring the matter to another judge for assignment as required by statute.

  3. Abuse of the "Frivolous" Standard: Whether a court violates the "factual frivoldusness" standard established in Denton v. Hernandez by "piercing the veil" of a complaint to find it "irrational" when the petitioner has provided twelve corroborating documents establishing the factual reality of the underlying incident.

  4. The Jurisdictional Characterization Error: Whether a court violates the Due Process Clause and the "less stringent standard" for pro se litigants (Haines v. Kerner) by reframing a complaint explicitly initiated under the Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt Organizations Act (RICO) as a standard 42 U.S.C. § 1983 claim to apply "state action" and Heckv. Humphrey bars that are inapplicable to racketeering claims.

  5. The "Counsel Mandate" Catch-22 (Rule 17): Whether the automatic dismissal of minor children's constitutional claims—solely because the indigent parent is proceeding pro se—violates the minor's right of meaningful access to the courts under the Fourteenth Amendment.
