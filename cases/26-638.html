Question presented

The Double Jeopardy Clause protects a defendant’s “valued right to have his trial completed by a particular tribunal.” Arizona v. Washington, 434 U.S. 497, 503 (1978). When a mistrial is declared over the defendant’s objection, the prosecution bears the burden of establishing the “high degree” of necessity sufficient to permit a second trial. Id. at 505–06. When alleged attorney misconduct during opening statement is asserted to have prejudiced the jury, Washington directs particular attention to “the likelihood that the impartiality of one or more jurors may have been affected.” Id. at 511. The question presented is: Whether, under 28 U.S.C. § 2254(d)(1), a state court reasonably applies the Double Jeopardy Clause’s manifest-necessity standard when it permits retrial after a mistrial declared over the defendant’s objection where, before the jury was discharged, the defendant offered to admit the disputed evidence in full, and the trial court maintained the mistrial because of the prosecution’s asserted strategic disadvantage and defense counsel’s responsibility for creating the problem rather than prejudice that prevented the jury from fairly deciding the case.
