Joseph J. Viola, aka Giuseppe Viola v. United States

IFP petition · United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, No. 17-15726 · judgment November 8, 2017

Certiorari denied · May 21, 2018

Question presented

Eight years ago, beginning in March 2010, a set of three closely interrelated proceedings were used by their adversary parties to deprive this petitioner of a life’s work, his assets, and perhaps most importantly, of his constitutional right to due process. During all that time, the petitioner has been held in continuous custody, through a demonstrable co-ordination among those involved, where the purposefully separated proceedings have been utilized to frustrate his efforts to achieve relief. Those proceedings, in the order of their initiation, as herein enumerated, using their Ninth Circuit docket numbers, include an application to proceed in forma pauperis in (1) No. 16-16556, on appeal of an involuntary bankruptcy; and for certificates of appealability in both (2) No. 16-15253, a review on habeas corpus under 28 USC § 2254 from an indictment issued in Arizona twenty years earlier, alleging events occurring between 1987 and 1989 for a distinct and distinguishable individual with the same common Italian surname; and (3) No. 17-15726, a review under § 2255 of a motion to vacate federal charges resulting after having been extradited from his residence and businesses of over two decades in San Francisco. Since finally managing to docket concurrent appeals for all of the three matters in the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, those efforts have included motions in each to consolidate the three into a single appeal, for the purposes of presenting a more complete understanding of their collective development, only from which a meaningful and proper resolution might result, and for judicial economy. Most recently, however, these threshold appellate efforts have been subverted by deputy clerks acting in the name of Article III judges under whose authority they were designated to assist, but instead, have usurped such power beyond that delegated to them, which might be mistaken for final dispositions.
