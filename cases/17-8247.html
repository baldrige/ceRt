David M. Donovan v. Texas

IFP petition · Court of Criminal Appeals of Texas, No. WR-75,582-03 · judgment November 22, 2017

Certiorari denied · May 29, 2018

Question presented

In 1998, prior to entering a plea of nolo contendere in exchange for a plea bargain for five (5) years deferred adjudication probation for aggravated sexual assault of a child, Petitioner asked his counsel on at least three (3) seperate occasions whether he would have to register as a sex offender as a result of the plea bargain. Petitioner, was incorrectly advised by counsel the first two times that since "the D.A. didn't offer it," then Petitioner was "not going to have to do it." On the third time that Petitioner asked whether he would have to register as 'a sex offender, counsel stated "NO!" Unfortunately, because the offense was a sexually violent offense, and the deferred adjudication probation was a "reportable... adjudication," it triggered mandatory sex offender registration not only for the probation period but for life. Minutes after Petitioner's plea, he discovered that he would be required to register as a sex offender. Petitioner informed the Court Liason Officer and the trial court that he was unaware of the sex offender registration consequence, because counsel had misinformed him, and he wanted to withdraw his plea and go to trial. Counsel had already left the courtroom by then. The trial court refused to allow Petitioner to withdraw his plea but instead sentenced him to boot camp. Counsel filed a writ of habeas corpus the next day seeking to withdraw Petitioner's plea and set the matter for trial - although counsel had raised other issues of involuntariness when he prepared the writ of habeas corpus after speaking to Petitioner's family while Petitioner was in jail. Nine days after Petitioner's plea, his family ratained new counsel who filed a motion for new trial which the trial court refused. Petitioner sought both a direct appeal, which was affirmed in a 2-1 decision,. and a petition for discretionary review which was granted and affirmed in a 5-4 decision. Petitioner later sought post-conviction relief in the Coust of Criminal Appeals of Texas arguing that his attorney had failed to correctly advise him of the sex offender registration consequence. After a live evidentiary hearing in the trial court, where plea counsel acknowledged his misunderstanding of the law, and that he gave inaccurate advice regarding sex offender registration, the Court of Criminal Appeals denied postconviction relief without written order based on the findings of the trial court which held that there was "no guarantee" that Petitioner would have gone to trial had counsel correctly advised him, and there was "no guarantee" that Petitioner would not have been convicted in a trial. Petitioner now seeks certiorari to review the following questions: Does counsel have a contitutional duty to correctly advise a defendant of the sex offender registration consequences that stem from a plea bargain for probation, when the registration consequence is a major concern of the defendant's that counsel is aware of, the defendant makes several specific inquiries about registration, the registration consequence is statutorily mandated for life, and the grossly incorrect advice induced Petitioner's plea of nolo contendere Whether plea counsel's acknowledged failure to be abreast of the law and his gross misadvice to the defendant regarding sex offender registration consequences that stem from a plea bargain, being that the registration consequence is a major concern of the defendant's that counsel is aware of, and Petitioner made several specific inquiries about, is grounds for setting aside a nob contendere plea when this incorrect advice induced Petitioner's plea Did counsel have a duty to correctly advise Petitioner concerning his numerous specific inquiries relating to his major concerns of sex offender registration consequences-when a majority of Federal Circuit Courts of Appeals have held that when it comes to parole eligibility counsel had a duty to correctly advise the defendant regarding their specific inquiries and concerns (4) Whether a trial court and the Court of Criminal Appeals of Texas can overrule and replace this Court's standard of "reasonable probability" with their own standard of "no guarantee"
