Latosha A. Ward v. Tony E. Ward, Sr., et al.

IFP petition · United States Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit, No. 25-2131 · judgment April 27, 2026

Pending

Questions presented

  1. Whether a final appellate judgment satisfies its inherent custodial duty to preserve the absolute integrity of the judicial system violates procedural due process by treating systemic automated docket alterations as non-punishable machine glitches or "harmless error" without conducting a human unauthenticated, Al-hallucinated electronic records onto the official CM/ECF and PACER networks -led evidentiary hearing to determine whether the record pollution arose from structural automation defects or sanctionable human misconduct that violates the doctrine of fraud upon the court established in Hazel-Atlas Glass Co. v. Hartford-Empire Co., 322 U.S. 238

  2. and obscure a structural due process defect of a protected and verified Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) Successor in Interest status’s due process rights through the federal courts under the Fifth and Fourteenth Amendments.

  3. Whether retained corporate counsel commits extrinsic fraud upon the court under Hazel-Atlas Glass Co. v. Hartford-Empire Co., 322 U.S. 238 (1944), and inflicts a structural due process violation, by utilizing official PACER/CM-ECF identity­ access-management credentials to submit adversarial pleadings ghostwritten by a former appellate clerk of that same Circuit under a false, unrepresented pro se posture for the explicit purpose of evading automated conflict-of-interest screenings.

  4. Will the problem going forward under the uniform pleading thresholds of Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 12(b)(6) and the Civil RICO statute, 18 U.S.C. § 1962(c), disregard a complaint of a viable association-in-fact enterprise when it alleges that a coordinated, multi-party, multi-state, multi-industry network—consisting of a mortgage servicer, an insurance carrier, and a homeowners association—operated in concert to conceal corporate successor-by-merger acquisitions, bypass federal financial disclosures, and systematically divert real estate assets to shell companies to defeat due process rights of a protected divorced Veteran spouse who has verified documented Dept of Veterans Affairs (VA) Successor in Interest protected status?

  5. Whether a residential mortgage servicer’s nationwide practice of utilizing deliberate, intentional, unrecorded and noncompliant corporate successor-by-merger acquisitions to the federal court with the mandatory financial disclosure provisions of Supreme Court Rule 29.6—specifically the concealment of a multi-billion-dollar unrecorded acquisition during active litigation, bypassing mandatory SEC financial disclosures, and deploying structural processing blackouts to systematically evade due process Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) loan assumption mandates and violates CFPB OIG directives constitutes an unconstitutional deprivation and taking of protected housing assets under the Fifth and Fourteenth Amendments.

  6. Whether a federal district court must exercise federal question jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1331 to enjoin a federal district court property execution where the underlying action targets a protected housing asset and arises from a multi-state corporate enterprise's systemic violation of the Civil RICO statute, 18 U.S.C. § 1964(c), and uniform federal pleading standards.

  7. Whether the systemic modification, manipulation, or unauthorized alteration of court dockets and cause-of-action codes by court personnel or automated electronic templates constitutes a structural defect under Arizona v. Fulminante, 499 U.S. 279 (1991), that fundamentally denies a protected verified VA Successor of Interest meaningful, unhindered access to the federal courts, and only allows due process for federal Respondents from federal liability for multi-state property deprivation acts.
