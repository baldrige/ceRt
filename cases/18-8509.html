Towaki Komatsu v. NTT Data, Inc., et al.

IFP petition · United States Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit, No. 16-2977 · judgment July 12, 2018

Certiorari denied · May 13, 2019

Questions presented

Since the following is entirely true and accurate, how may it be credibly claimed that the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit didn’t substantially err when it issued its decision on 7/12/18 in which it affirmed the judgment of the U.S. District Court Judge Lorna Schofield in regards to my lower federal court lawsuit against the Respondents? New York State Judge Lisa Sokoloff caused an intervening and substantial change in controlling law to occur through decisions she issued on 11/28/16 and 6/9/17 in my state court lawsuit against NTT Data, Inc. (“NTT”). She did so by providing guidance in those decisions in which she stated that in the event that I sought to be granted equitable relief in regards to the decision that New York State Judge Jennifer Schecter issued on 4/11/14 in that case that compelled me to engage in arbitration with NTT, I would need to pursue such relief from a court that has the power to grant equitable relief Prior to Judge Sokoloff having issued those decisions in that case on those dates, I hadn’t been granted any such concession that would enable me to possibly be granted equitable relief in regards to Judge Schecters 4/11/14 decision. While I presented oral arguments to the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit on 5/22/18, I apprised the 3 judges who listened to my arguments of this substantial change in controlling law that fundamentally changed the dynamics of my pursuit of equitable relief in regards to Judge Schecter’s 4/11/14 decision by causing the Rooker-Feldman Doctrine to cease to apply and bar federal court intervention in regards to that matter. Exxon Mobil Corp. v. Saudi Basic Indus. Corp., 544 U.S. 280 (2005) includes pertinent findings that confirm that the Rooker-Feldman Doctrine does not bar federal courts from reviewing independent claims with respect to parallel state court litigation that I clearly apprised Judge Schofield about in a reply affidavit dated 9/25/15 that I filed in my lower federal court lawsuit against the respondents. How has it been possible for me to engage in arbitration with NTT, since I have never had the ability to pay for the costs of that arbitration, I was denied a hardship fee waiver by the American Arbitration Association (“AAA”), and I will not drop any legal claim against NTT in order for it to consider paying the entire costs of arbitration? Given the exceptions to the Younger Abstention Doctrine and 9 U.S.C. §2, why didn’t Judge Lorna Schofield act in accordance with the “Savings Clause” of the Federal Arbitration Act (“FAA”) by granting me declaratory and equitable relief pursuant to her authority to exercise supplemental jurisdiction in regards to the independent claim as to whether it was financially impossible for me to engage in arbitration with NTT that wasn’t barred by the Rooker-Feldman Doctrine in response to my having apprised her of the fact that Judge Schecter compelled me to engage in arbitration with NTT only after she demonstrated bad faith toward me in my state court lawsuit against NTT that hindsight confirms caused me substantial harm by having never attempted to satisfy a binding legal prerequisite that required her to first determine whether it was financially impossible for me to engage in arbitration with NTT prior to compelling me to do so?

  1. Since a) I previously worked at Credit Suisse in Japan during 2 separate periods prior to 2012 that enabled me to learn what its tendencies were insofar as how it managed personnel it classified as contractors as a result of my having personally experienced how it managed personnel it claimed were contractors as if they were its permanent employees and caused them to have been misclassified as contractors as a result and b) concluded my employment with Credit Suisse prior to 2012 in a distinctively acrimonious way with respect to managers at Credit Suisse in Japan primarily because such managers caused me to have been misclassified as a contractor, how can it be credibly claimed that I intended to resolve disputes thereafter through arbitration with Credit Suisse that were caused by adverse actions that Credit Suisse’s personnel committed in 2012 against me?

  2. Since the following decisions were issued in chronological order in the state court lawsuit that I commenced against NTT that is filed with the New York City Civil Court, how can it be credibly claimed that it was proper Judge Lisa Sokoloff and Judge Jennifer Schecter that were assigned to that lawsuit to refuse to vacate the decision that was issued by Judge Schecter on 4/11/14 that granted a motion that an attorney for NTT filed to compel me to engage in arbitration with NTT? Judge Peter Moulton issued a decision on 7/15/13 that denied a motion that an attorney for NTT filed to compel me to engage in arbitration with NTT. Judge Debra Rose Samuels issued a decision on 8/14/13 that vacated Judge Moulton’s decision. Judge Schecter issued her decision on 4/11/14 that compelled me to engage in arbitration with NTT. Judge Schecter denied an Order to Show Cause application on 10/6/15 while expressing remarks in which she claimed that that she hadn’t overlooked anything when issuing her decision on 4/11/14.

  3. Since the following material facts are true about people who are victims of both wage-theft and employment blacklisting, how inappropriate was it that Judge Schofield and the United States Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit (“2’ Circuit”) gave short shrift to the arguments that I presented in my litigation in the lower courts that I should be granted equitable tolling in regards to statutory deadlines for my claims against NTT and Credit Suisse because I had been experiencing these precise hardships and continued to do so due to NTT and Credit Suisse’s illegal acts against me in 2012 that were the root cause of those problems and that caused equitable estoppel to clearly apply? a. People who are victims of both wage-theft and employment blacklisting are invariably caused by those circumstances to be at a significantly higher risk of experiencing substantial collateral harm such as severe difficulty with being able - to pay rent for their apartments and medical bill. Such collateral harm may cause those victims to have to contend with litigation against their landlords due to difficulties with paying rent. Such litigation causes those victims to be absent from jobs to attend court hearings, as those repeated absences cause their employers to cause those victims to lose those jobs. The circumstances just described fuel a snowball effect and vicious cycle that ultimately leads to causing the victims to become homeless and have to contend with the extraordinary and arguably irreparable problems that causes those victims to experience. After NTT’s personnel committed acts of wire fraud against me in 2012 in violation of 18 U.s.c. §1343, when Francis Convery of NTT in his official capacity as NTT’s Chief Operating Officer caused new acts of mail fraud and wire fraud to be committed against me on 9/23/15 by making fraudulent remarks in a sworn affidavit that was filed by an attorney for NTT electronically in my lower court litigation against the respondents that was assigned to Judge Schofield and she ignored the clearly obvious perjury Mr. Convery committed by having made those fraudulent remarks, how can it be credibly claimed that those illegal acts were not ripe for me to pursue claims against NTT pursuant to 18 U.S.C. 1964 and 18 U.S.C. §1514A while equitable tolling continued to be warranted for me largely because of the hardships that I was suffering due to respondents’ illegal acts against me? After Judge Sokoloff issued a decision on 6/9/17 in my state court lawsuit against NTT in which she explicitly directed me to reach out to a Supreme Court to seek equitable relief in relation to having to engage in arbitration with NTT and lied while making the following pertinent remarks, why shouldn’t this Court equitably remedy her errors and those of Judge Schecter by declaring that the arbitration provision that NTT included in the contract that it issued that enabled me to work at Credit Suisse in New York City in 2012 has proven to be unenforceable because of both my proven inability to pay for the costs of engaging in arbitration with NTT, my proven inability to be granted a hardship fee waiver by the AAA, and my refusal to forego pursuing any legal claims against NTT in order for it to consider paying for the costs of arbitration between it and I?
