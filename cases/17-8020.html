Darrell Bryan v. Kentucky

IFP petition · Supreme Court of Kentucky, No. 2015-SC-000467-MR · judgment March 23, 2017

Certiorari denied · April 23, 2018

Question presented

Where petitioner received a Fifty (50) year sentence for capital murder based upon incomplete self-defense instructions, the petitioner was denied exculpatory jury instructions for the “imperfect self-defense” pursuant to KRS 503.120 (1). The Supreme Court of Kentucky agreed unanimously (7-0), that it was in fact error to exclude this exculpatory instruction in a capital murder trial, however, the bare majority found it to be harmless error to do so. Chief Justice Minton dissented, joined by Justices Keller and Wright. Should the Court of last resort be able to deny a unanimously agreed upon violation of this petitioner’s 6th and 14th Amendments right to present a defense based on that majority’s designation of “harmless error,” or be held to the standard set out in Chapman v. California, by this United States Supreme Court, of “harmless beyond a reasonable doubt”(?) Will that preserve this petitioner’s rights to Due Process? Where appellant raised specific Constitutional Violations of the 5th, 6th, and 14th Amendments, for the prolonged display of this petitioner in prison garb, handcuffs, and shackles during the guilt phase of his capital murder trial for an excess of Thirty (30) plus minutes, via this petitioner’s video statement. This was over trial counsel’s objection. There was no admonition given to disregard the prison garb and chains and the video was played. Can the Court of last resort be fair in its analysis to determine that the error was harmless under the 5th, 6th, and 14th Amendment violations displaying this petitioner in prison garb and chains, which is contrary to the United States precedent under Deck v. Missouri, where Justice Kennedy states, “Through control of a defendant’s appearance, the state can exert a powerful influence on the outcome of the trial’(.) as harmless error?
