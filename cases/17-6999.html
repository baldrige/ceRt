Sentoryia Young v. Tony Mays, Warden

IFP petition · United States Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit, No. 16-5075 · judgment July 14, 2017

Certiorari denied · January 16, 2018

Question presented

Every federal judge who has reviewed the performance of postconviction appellate counsel in this case agree that his performance was ineffective and warrants action by the bar. Nonetheless, the federal courthouse doors remain closed to Petitioner’s substantial claims of constitutional error because the derelict attorney defaulted them without ever communicating with Petitioner. In Maples v. Thomas, 565 U.S. 266. 283 (2012) this Court held, “under agency principles, a client cannot be charged with the acts or omissions of an attorney who has abandoned him. Nor can a client be faulted for failing to act on his own behalf when he lacks reason to believe his attorneys of record, in fact, are not representing him.” This Court has expressed particular concern where a petitioner is precluded from federal review of a claim of ineffective assistance of trial counsel due to postconviction counsel’s deficient performance because the “right to the effective assistance of counsel at trial is a bedrock principle in our justice system.” Martinez v. Ryan, 566 U.S. 1, 12(2012). In Martinez, this Court acknowledged its considerable discretion to act in equity to find cause to excuse procedural default when justice demands. Id., at 13. The Questions Presented are: Does the court’s ruling in Maples v. Thomas, 565 U.S. 266 (2012), that an attorney’s abandonment of a client may provide cause to excuse a procedural default, require complete abandonment of the client, as the Sixth Circuit held, or effective abandonment, as the Eleventh and Ninth circuits hold? Should the equitable principles articulated in Martinez v. Ryan, 566 U.S. 1(20 12), and Trevino v. Thaler, 569 U.S. 413 (2013), that ineffective assistance of trial level post-conviction counsel can supply cause to excuse a procedural default in federal habeas corpus review apply with equal force to ineffective assistance of counsel by appellate post-conviction counsel? When predecessor counsel refuses to be interviewed regarding his representation of a habeas petitioner, and there is substantial evidence of abandonment by that predecessor counsel, is a habeas petition entitled in discovery to depose predecessor counsel to make a complete record of predecessor counsel’s abandonment?
