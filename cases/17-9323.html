In Re Stephanie Michael

IFP petition

Dismissed · December 13, 2018

Question presented

This case presents deep issues of national importance and arises from rules of law established by seriously erroneous interpretation of statutory language of the Veterans Judicial Review Act of 1988 and 38 U.S.C. §511 of the Department of Veterans Affairs codification Act of 1991 by the lower courts which undermines congressional policy as expressed in other legislation. congress speaks directly to the precise questions of law of its unambiguous legislative intent for judicial review of the administrative actions of the VA under laws affecting the provision of benefits. VA regulations 38 CFR §1.579 and 38 CFR §20.1201 promulgates the procedure for Michael civil remedy under 5 U.S.C. §552a of the Privacy Act relevant law applicable to the individual record relied upon in Board of Veterans Appeals benefit determinations. 38 CFR §14.600-14.605 establishes procedure for Michael's claim colorable under 28 USC §1346(b) and advises that "an action against the United States under 28 U.S.C. §2671- 2680 is the exclusive remedy under these circumstances." Yet, void judgments here impose Bivens remedy of Zuspann v. Brown, 60 F.3d 1156 (1995) applying the rule of law: "Section 511 of Title 38, formerly §211, precludes judicial review of veterans' benefits determinations ... Congress passed the Veterans' Judicial Review Act ("VJRA"), which clearly announced the intent of congress to preclude review of benefits determinations in federal district courts." In re Wick, 40 F.3d 367 (Fed. cir. 1994) here diminishes the jurisdiction U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans claims establishing a rule of law that the Court of the united States lacks authority to issue judgment against the United States under 28 U.S.C. § 2201 of the Declaratory Judgment Act. The questions presented are: Whether judicial actions of the U.S. District court and the U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims are void when such judgments obtained by fraudulent conduct of officers of the Court expand or diminish the jurisdiction of a Federal Court and subsequently modify, abridge, or enlarge the substantive and legislative rights of litigants in a manner that undermines congressional policy applicable to judicial review of the administrative agency actions that adversely affect the provision of VA benefits. Whether mandamus action is appropriate under the unusual and rare circumstance that arises when a rule of law must be renounced to correct seriously erroneous interpretations of legislative intent of statutory language by the lower Courts in the absence of plenary guidance of the Supreme Court is exacerbated by improper conduct of officers of the Court.
