Kendle Rashen Hawkins v. United States

IFP petition · United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, No. 24-7781 · judgment May 28, 2026

Pending

Question presented

Since its landmark decision in Wong Sun v. United States, 371 U.S. 471 (1963), this Court has held to the basic principle that, with limited exceptions, unlawfully obtained evidence may not be exploited to obtain further evidence, even if the subsequent evidence is itself lawfully obtained. The Court has also held, in Whren v. United States, 517 U.S. 806 (1996), that a law enforcement officer may detain a motorist to investigate a traffic infraction, regardless of whether the officer’s true motive is to investigate a wholly different offense, so long as there is probable cause to believe a traffic violation was committed. This case lies at the intersection of these two principles. The question presented here is: Does the fact that a traffic stop is lawful under Whren automatically purge the taint of a location tracking warrant that was not supported by probable cause but led officers to the vehicle, or must a court engage in an individualized analysis of whether the traffic stop was attenuated from, or independent of, the deficient warrant?
