Question presented

Under 28 U.S.C. § 2461(a), the government must bring “a civil action” to collect any civil penalty “prescribed for the violation of an Act of Congress,” unless Congress “specif[ies]” a different “mode of recovery.” Congress sometimes deviates from that default rule. For instance, Congress has made certain Tax Code penalties “assessable,” meaning the IRS can collect them using its lien and levy powers without going to court. The IRS asserts that one such provision is I.R.C. § 6038(b), which provides for a $10,000 penalty—increasable to $60,000—for each year in which a taxpayer fails to report control of a foreign business. Two courts of appeals and the Tax Court have split over whether § 6038(b) penalties are assessable. The Tax Court has repeatedly held—including in a decision joined by 16 of 17 judges—that § 2461(a)’s default rule requires the government to use a civil action to collect § 6038(b) penalties, because the Tax Code doesn’t make those penalties assessable. E.g., Mukhi v. Commissioner, 163 T.C. 150, 162 (2024). The Tax Court has expressly stated that, until this Court weighs in, the IRS cannot assess § 6038(b) penalties nationwide except in circuits, with which it disagrees, holding the penalties assessable. Id. at 154. The D.C. and Second Circuits disagree. Those courts hold that § 6038(b) penalties are assessable based on their notions of congressional purpose, even though they admit the Tax Code is silent on the question. App. 6a7a; Farhy v. Commissioner, 100 F.4th 223, 230-31 (D.C. Cir. 2024). The question presented is: Whether the government must use a civil action under 28 U.S.C. § 2461(a), rather than assessment, to collect I.R.C. § 6038(b) penalties.
