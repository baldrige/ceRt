James D. Johnson v. Scott R. Frakes, Director, Nebraska Department of Correctional Services

IFP petition · United States Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit, No. 17-1700 · judgment September 7, 2017

Certiorari denied · June 11, 2018

Question presented

The following five case specific Questions Presented aside, this petition generally asks: WHETHER the tenets of the First Amendment's Petition Clause are met by permitting the civil death of prisoners. WHETHER the entitlement of prisoners to "procedures appropriate under the circumstances and required by the Due Process Clause to ensure that the state created right [to good time credit] is not arbitrarily abrogated," as found in Wolff V. McDonnell, 418 U.S. 539, 557 (1974), can extend beyond administrative procedures within prisons and into the purview of whether state court denials of due process in constructions and applications of states' prison good time credit statutes arbitrarily abrogate the states' created rights; and/or: WHETHER state court statutory constructions and applications which arbitrarily abrogate states' created rights to prison good time credit are "obvious subterfuge[s] to evade consideration of a federal issue," see Radio Station WOW v. Johnson, 326 U.S. 120, 129 (1945), making them "circumstance[s which may] be subject to review by the federal courts." See, e.g., Breedlove v. Moore, 421 F.3d 952, 964 n. 8 (11th Cir. 2002) (citing Mullaney v. Wilbur, 421 U.S. 684 (1975)); and, if so, WHETHER, in federal habeas cases alleging liberty deprivations caused by unconstitutional state court constructions and applications of state prison good time credit statutes, U.S. District Court decisions to dismiss same, with prejudice, for lack of federal claims violate prisoners' "constitutional right[s] to petition the Government for redress of their grievances, which includes a reasonable right of access to the courts." See Hudson v. Palmer, 468 U.S. 517, 523 (1984) (citing Johnson v. Avery, 393 U.S. 483 (1969), cf. the First Amendment's Petition Clause); and, if so, WHETHER, when the exhaustion of states' provided remedies requirement in 28 U.S.C. § 2254(b) is taken into account, a U.S. District court dismissal with prejudice of a § 2254 habeas application (by a person/prisoner in state custody) alleging deprivation of liberty caused by unconstitutional state supreme court constructions and applications of a state law, with such dismissal due to lack of a federal claim premised upon the U.S. District court's assertion that federal habeas courts lack power to review state supreme.coürt:decisions, it amounts to erecting an arbitrary barrier to reaching the merits, and erecting arbitrary barriers to reaching the habeas applicant's merits violates the prisoner's "reasonable right of access to the courts," id., and is an unconstitutional form of civil death for state prisoners; and, if so, WHETHER, when in a state prisoner's federal habeas application alleging deprivation of liberty caused by unconstitutional state court constructions and applications of a state law the U.S. District court erects arbitrary barriers to reaching the prisoner's merits and dismisses the case with prejudice for lack of a federal claim, refusals by the U.S. District court, on motion to alter or amend, and by the circuit court of Appeals, on request and petition for rehearing, to issue the certificate of Appealability required by 28 U.S.C. § 2253(c) and Fed. R. App. P. 22(b)(1) before the prisoner/applicant may appeal the dismissal of his case violates his "reasonable right of access to the courts," id., and ensures the unconstitutional form of civil death inherent to refusals by courts to reach the merits of prisoners' cases and/or courts' erecting arbitrary barriers to doing so.
