Kenneth Wayne Lancaster v. Oregon

IFP petition · Court of Appeals of Oregon, No. A178488 · judgment November 26, 2025

Pending

Question presented

Petitioner Kenneth Wayne Lancaster was convicted, following a guilty plea, of first-degree rape, first-degree sodomy, first-degree unlawful sexual penetration, and first-degree sexual abuse. At sentencing, the trial court imposed mandatory minimum sentences under Oregon’s Ballot Measure 11 sentencing scheme, ORS 137.700, totaling an aggregate 600 months (50 years) of imprisonment. Petitioner presented undisputed evidence that he suffers from post-traumatic stress disorder and borderline personality disorder, diagnosed conditions resulting from documented severe childhood physical and sexual abuse, and that those conditions directly affected his capacity to control his conduct. The trial court imposed the mandatory sentences without according that evidence any weight in assessing petitioner’s culpability. The Oregon Court of Appeals affirmed, holding that Article 1, section 16, of the Oregon Constitution requires no different result, and disposed of petitioner’s parallel Eighth Amendment claim on the same reasoning. The Oregon Supreme Court denied review. The question presented is: Whether the Eighth Amendment’s prohibition on cruel and unusual punishment requires a sentencing court, before imposing a mandatory minimum term under a statutory sentencing scheme that permits no downward departure, to consider undisputed evidence that a defendant’s capacity to control his conduct was diminished by diagnosed mental health conditions resulting from documented childhood trauma - or whether, as the courts below held, such evidence may be categorically disregarded absent proof that the defendant’s condition rendered him the functional equivalent of a child. Form 42.010 PARTIES TQ THE PROCEEDING Petitioner Kenneth Wayne Lancaster was the defendant-appellant below. Respondent State of Oregon was the plaintiff-respondent below. There are no other parties.
