Shakeel A. Kahn v. United States

IFP petition · United States Court of Appeals for the Tenth Circuit, No. 25-8004 · judgment August 20, 2026

Pending

Questions presented

The Controlled Substances Act makes dispensing controlled substances unlawful “[e]xcept as authorized by this subchapter.” 21 U.S.C. § 841(a)(1). Section 822(b) grants registered practitioners authority subject to their registration and the statute. An administrative regulation, 21 C.F.R. § 1306.04(a), separately defines an “effective” prescription by reference to “legitimate medical purpose” and the “usual course” of professional practice. In Ruan v. United States, 597 U.S. 450, 455 (2022), this Court assumed the regulatory definition while deciding the required mens rea. The court below held that United States v. Moore, 423 U.S. 122 (1975), foreclosed petitioner’s challenge to that definition. The questions presented are:

  1. Whether Moore should be reconsidered and overruled or limited in light of West Virginia v. EPA, 597 U.S. 697 (2022), and Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo, 603 U.S. 369 (2024), because § 1306.04(a) cannot define the scope of criminal culpability under § 841 independently of the statute, and Ruan’s knowledge requirement does not cure that defect.

  2. Whether, independently of the regulation’s use to define a criminal offense, the Attorney General exceeded the authority delegated by the CSA in promulgating § 1306.04(a) as a substantive medical-practice limitation on a registered practitioner’s prescribing authority.
