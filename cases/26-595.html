Questions presented

In Ames v. Ohio Department of Youth Services, this Court “assume[d] without deciding that the McDonnell Douglas framework applies at the summary-judgment stage of litigation.” 605 U.S. 303, 308 n.2 (2025). The courts of appeals regularly use the framework at summary judgment but disagree about whether doing so is mandatory. Some require plaintiffs lacking direct evidence of discrimination to proceed through the framework; others permit them to instead rely on the whole record outside the framework. Two Members of this Court have also expressed willingness to reconsider its use at summary judgment. See id. at 319 (Thomas, J., joined by Gorsuch, J., concurring). The questions presented are:

  1. Whether a Title VII plaintiff relying on circumstantial evidence must satisfy the three-step framework of McDonnell Douglas Corp. v. Green, 411 U.S. 792 (1973), to survive summary judgment, or may instead establish a triable claim by showing that the evidence as a whole would permit a reasonable jury to find intentional discrimination.

  2. Whether McDonnell Douglas should be overruled or held inapplicable at summary judgment in Title VII cases.
