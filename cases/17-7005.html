Juliette Fairley v. Mauricette Fairley, et al.

IFP petition · Court of Appeals of Texas, Fourth District, No. 04-16-00096-CV · judgment January 18, 2017

Certiorari denied · January 16, 2018

Question presented

I. Whether the Fourth Court of Appeals, in denying Petitioner’s Motion for Rehearing, erred when it affirmed the trial court’s elimination of Petitioner’s legal standing. (2, 6) Whether the Fourth Court of Appeals erred when it decided against Petitioner’s appeal based on a lack of testimony disqualifying Respondent when Petitioner was barred from participating in legal proceedings. (3, 4, 6) Whether the Fourth Court of Appeals denied Due Process when it affirmed the trial court’s order that struck Petitioner’s legal standing because she did not fund the trial court with $20,000.1 (6) Whether requiring staggered cash payments of $10,000, $5,000 then $5,000 totaling $20,000 based on the outcome of future court proceedings constitutes a violation of Due Process of law and federal racketeering “Pay for Play” statutes.2 (7) Whether requiring only one applicant, in this case the Petitioner, to pay $20,000 in cash and not all applicants is a violation of the 14th Amendment’s Equal Protection Clause. Whether requiring Petitioner to pay $50 an hour to visit her father and limiting face to face visitation to four hours a month violates Petitioner’s Constitutional Right to Freedom of Association. (12) Whether requiring any applicant to pay $20,000 at all violated the 13th Amendment of the U.S. Constitution because it placed a price tag on the person of James Fairley. (7) The Texas Rules of Civil Procedure Rule 143 does not authorize the court to fix bond in a specific amount. It must be open-ended as litigated in Dilmore v. Russell and Mosher v. Tunnell. 2 18 U.S. Code § 1961 is any act indictable under the following provisions of title 18, United States Code: Section 201 (relating to bribery) and sections 1581-1592 (relating to peonage, slavery, and trafficking in persons)
