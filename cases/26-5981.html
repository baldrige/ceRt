Lynn M. Kipp v. Bellefonte Area School District, et al.

IFP petition · Commonwealth Court of Pennsylvania, No. 263 C.D. 2023 · judgment October 7, 2025

Pending

Questions presented

Within weeks of reporting concerns about state-mandated testing violations, a tenured, distinguished-rated public school teacher was suspended and told she was found to have violated several unlawful harassment policies. From the outset, she asked who had accused her and what she was alleged to have said or done. Her school district refused this information by calling the matter “confidential” under the policy. HR’s report—given to her eight months later—indicated names of complainants and alle­ gations that were unrelated to the unlawful harassment policy. Within that year she was suspended four times, again without names of her accusers or evidence or infor­ mation of what she said or did wrong. Nevertheless, the state courts held that the meetings at which she was refused that information gave her due process, and they dismissed her § 1983 complaint on the pleadings. The questions presented are:

  1. Whether, when a public employer suspends a tenured employee without pay, here indefinitely and now for more than seven years, on the strength of cowork­ ers’ accusations, the pre-suspension “notice of the charges” and “explanation of the employer’s evidence” required by Cleveland Board of Education v. Loudermill, 470 U.S. 532, 546 (1985), and applied to suspensions in Schmidt v. Creedon, 639 F.3d 587 (3d Cir. 2011), must disclose who the accusers are and what the employee is alleged to have said or done wrong; and whether meet­ ings at which the employer withholds both, citing a local confidentiality policy, satisfy due process under the Fourteenth Amendment.

  2. Whether it violates the First and Fourteenth Amendments for a public employer to invoke a harassment policy’s confidentiality clause both to withhold from an employee the identity of her accusers and the substance of the accusations, and to forbid and punish her efforts to gather witnesses and character evidence in her own defense, and then for state courts to seal from the public record the allegations unrelated to the unlawful harassment policy.

  3. Whether a court may dismiss her retaliation and association claims on the pleadings by treating the employer’s own charging document as proof that it would have acted anyway, contrary to Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle, 429 U.S. 274 (1977).

  4. Whether a state court may dismiss a public employee’s § 1983 due process claims as untimely under a state open-meetings statute’s 30-day limit, or be­ cause a union-controlled grievance arbitration was incomplete, when this Court has held that § 1983 claims are governed by the State’s general personal-injury limitations period, Owens v. Okure, 488 U.S. 235 (1989), and may not be con­ ditioned on state exhaustion or notice requirements, Patsy v. Board of Regents, 457 U.S. 496 (1982); Felder v. Casey, 487 U.S. 131 (1988).
