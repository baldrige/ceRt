Deandre D. Daniels v. Brad Johnson, Warden

IFP petition · United States Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit, No. 25-2964 · judgment May 26, 2026

Pending

Questions presented

A federal habeas petitioner whose petition is untimely may nonetheless obtain review of his constitutional claims if he makes a credible showing of actual innocence. McQuiggin v. Perkins, 569 U.S. 383 (2013); Schlup v. Delo, 513 U.S. 298 (1995). Under Schlup and House v. Bell, 547 U.S. 518 (2006), the habeas court must make a holistic, independent assessment of all the evidence, old and new, and determine how reasonable jurors would likely react to it. In House, this Court expressly declined to decide whether 28 U.S.C. § 2254(e)(1), which presumes state4 court factual findings correct absent clear and convincing evidence, applies to that gateway inquiry. The courts of appeals have since divided over the answer, and in this case the courts below applied § 2254(e)(1) deference to state-court credibility findings to reject Petitioner's gateway innocence showing. The questions presented are:

  1. Whether 28 U.S.C. § 2254(e)(l)'s presumption of correctness applies to state-court factual and credibility determinations when a federal habeas court evaluates a gateway claim of actual innocence under Schlup v. Delo, or whether the court must instead make the independent, holistic assessment of all the evidence that Schlup and House v. Bell require.

  2. Whether the court of appeals contravened Buck v. Davis, 580 U.S. 100 (2017), and Miller-El v. Cockrell, 537 U.S. 322 (2003), by denying a certificate of appealability rather than limiting itself to the threshold question whether reasonable jurists could debate the district court's procedural ruling, where the district court itself acknowledged that the first question presented is open in this Court and in the Seventh Circuit.
