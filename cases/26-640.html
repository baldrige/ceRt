In Re Paul Berry, III

Questions presented

  1. Whether orders entered by a single district judge in an action challenging the apportionment of a State’s congressional districts — including an order finally disposing of the action — are void when the district judge was required to notify the chief judge of the circuit under 28 U.S.C. § 2284(b)(1), and as direct result, no three-judge district court was convened in the manner required by 28 U.S.C. § 2284(a).

  2. Whether a writ of mandamus should issue under the All Writs Act, 28 U.S.C. § 1651(a), where the district court sua sponte held a verified complaint’s emergency applications in abeyance and then dismissed the action on the eve of a federal election, leaving the petitioner with no other adequate means to attain the relief in any court.

  3. Whether a writ of mandamus should issue under the All Writs Act, 28 U.S.C. § 1651(a), where the appellate court held the mandate to its order to the district court requiring the notice of appeal directed by the petitioner to this Court be transmitted to this Court until the eve of a federal election and at a temporal period in violation Rule 41 of the Federal Rules of Appellate Procedure, leaving the petitioner with no other adequate means to attain appellate relief in any court in a timely manner.

  4. Whether, because Missouri has not been redistricted in the manner provided by law following the most recent apportionment, 2 U.S.C. § 2a(c)(5) requires Missouri to elect its Representatives to the Congress from the State at large in the 2026 general election.
