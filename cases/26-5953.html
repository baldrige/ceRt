William Meece v. John R. Grise, Judge, Circuit Court of Kentucky, Warren County, et al.

IFP petition · Supreme Court of Kentucky, No. 2025-SC-0299-OA · judgment February 19, 2026

Pending

Question presented

In 2013, Mr. Meece filed a motion for post-conviction relief in Kentucky state courts, challenging multiple convictions and sentences of death. As required by Kentucky law, Mr. Meece’s counsel attached along with that petition a verification of the petition signed by Mr. Meece. In an effort to meet Kentucky’s pleading requirements and avoid summary dismissal, counsel also attached several affidavits, including an affidavit of Mr. Meece himself. Twelve years later, during penalty-phase post-conviction evidentiary hearings in which Mr. Meece chose not to testify, the Commonwealth notified the parties of its intent to call Mr. Meece as it’s sole witness. On a writ, the Kentucky Supreme Court ruled that Mr. Meece had waived his Fifth Amendment privilege in submitting the affidavit and petition verification, and that the Commonwealth could therefore call him to testify. As a basis for its ruling, the court cited to Mitchell v. United States, 526 U.S. 314 (1999) for the proposition that, “a witness, in a single proceeding, may not testify voluntarily about a subject and then invoke the privilege against self-incrimination when questioned about the details.” Pet. App. 6a. While this Court in Mitchell v. United States did “note” (Id. at 323) the general rule cited by the Kentucky Supreme Court, it decidedly narrowed its application, ruling that a limited waiver for one portion of criminal proceedings does not constitute a broad waiver of all Fifth Amendment privileges for subsequent proceedings which are distinct in purpose from the proceeding in which the testimony was given. Id., at 323-325.
