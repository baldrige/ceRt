Questions presented

Petitioner Joy Ho Scherer was beaten, and nearly raped, by a man she had been dating. Bloody and visibly injured, she told Officer Nelson Martinez that she wanted to press charges. Her attacker said that he did not. Officer Martinez then told Scherer: “if he wants to press charges, you will be going to jail too.” In a prior appeal, the Ninth Circuit held that Scherer plausibly alleged that this statement “was a threat, not a statement of law,” and that it “would have chilled a person of ordinary firmness from exercising the First Amendment right to press charges.” App. 30a. A divided panel nevertheless granted qualified immunity because Scherer cited no case “clearly establishing a First Amendment violation in a situation like this one, where a police officer is responding to the scene of a domestic assault and advising both parties about their respective rights to press charges”—even when those “advisals” are “plausibly regarded as threats.” App. 4a. The questions presented:

  1. Whether, under Hope v. Pelzer, 536 U.S. 730 (2002), an officer whose threat of arrest would chill a crime victim from exercising her First Amendment right to press charges is entitled to qualified immunity because no prior decision involved materially similar facts.

  2. Whether, in deciding qualified immunity on a motion to dismiss, a court may define the “specific context” of an officer’s conduct by drawing inferences in the officer’s favor, contrary to Tolan v. Cotton, 572 U.S. 650 (2014).
