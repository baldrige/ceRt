Pamela S. Owen v. Chuck E. Atkins, Sheriff, Clark County, Washington, et al.

IFP petition · United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, No. 16-35398 · judgment August 16, 2017

Certiorari denied · January 22, 2018

Question presented

Does the State of Washington violate basic principles of constitutional due process of law when its courts hobbles, ad hoc, State statutes and unknown court procedures to create the existence of jurisdiction to support the issuance of exparte default orders and judgments that conveys Petitioner's superior interest or estate without notice and a hearing, or does the limitations of the Fourth, Fifth and Fourteenth Amendment and this Court's holdings in Brown v. Barry, 3 U.S. (3 Dali.) 365 (1797) and Mullane v. Central Hanover Bank & Trust Co., 339 U.S. 306, 70 S. Ct. 652 (1950) no longer apply? Does the magnitude of the holdings of the Federal District Court and Court of Appeals violate Article III and the Fourth and Fifth Amendments of the Federal Constitution when finding no Federal constitutional due process and "state action" civil rights issues are presented because Petitioner remains in possession of her home, or does the limitations of U.S. Const. Article III and the Fifth Amendment and this Court's holdings in Stacy v. Thrasher, 47 U.S. 6 How. 44, 60 (1848); Shelley v. Kraemer, 334 U.S. 1, 13-14 (1948); Sniadach v. Family Fin. Corp., 395 U.S. 337, 338-39 (1969); Fuentes v. Shevin, 407 U.S. 67, 70-71 (1972); North Georgia Finishing, Incorporated v. Di-Chem, Inc., 419 U.S. 601 (1975); Lugar v. Edmondson Oil Company, 467 U.S. 922, 934, 940-42 (1980); and Edmondson v. Leesville Concrete Company, 500 U.S. 614 (1991) no longer apply? Do the decisions of the Federal District Court and Court of Appeals, granting summary judgment to Respondent Sheriff on grounds unrelated to the Sheriffs pleading of qualified immunity, violate this Court's doctrinal concepts that qualified immunity protects an official from trial and the burdens of litigation and discovery, including amendment of the action, or does Title 28 U.S.C. § 2679(b)(2); Rules 1 and 15, Federal Rules of Civil Procedure (Fed. R. Civ. P.) and the Court's holdings in Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800, 807 (1982); Butz v. Econ,omou, 438 U.S. 478 (1978) and Mitchell v. Forsyth, 472 U.S. 511, 526 (1985), no longer apply? Does the gravity of the dismissal of Petitioner's original complaint by the Federal District Court and Court of Appeals, as a sanction for failure to amend, operate to deny constitutional due process when considered in the light of the posture of the case stated in the three previous issues, or does U.S. Const. Article III, the Fourth and Fifth Amendments and Fed. R. Civ. P., Rules 1 and 15110 longer apply?
