David Petersen v. United States

IFP petition · United States Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit, No. 16-15603 · judgment September 13, 2017

Certiorari denied · January 8, 2018

Question presented

Can the Government mislead a defendant as to the completeness of SEC discovery provided to the defendant-by them, then represent on the record that all information received by them from the SEC was provided to the defendant, then imply that the said SEC investigation was ongoing and no final determination existed, when in fact they knew and received the final investigation findings and enforcement action documents three month prior to trial, then under the claim of lack of "reasonable diligence" by the defense deny the defendant use of this evidence to grant Petitioner's Rule 33 Motion as it impacted all major points of the Indictment against the defendant? Can the Government, by their concealment of evidence that also conceals the existence of an unknown witness favorable to the accused, violate the compulsory process rights of the accused? Concerning the unanswered constitutional jurisdiction claims made by the Petitioner in his Rule 33 Motion, and subsequent appeal and appeal briefs, that were not answered by the lower courts: Does the SEC's appointment by Congress as the "exclusive" federal agency to determine if when and by whom securities law violations have taken place (primary jurisdiction) extend to all federal criminal securities law offenses tried in Federal Courts? Can the Government proceed in a securities case, without any security on record, no privity to any security and no enforcement action from the SEC, and still have subject-matter jurisdiction if no other "case or controversy" is present? 4. Can lower courts ignore ruling on a constitutional jurisdiction claim, or ignore Federal Criminal Procedures Rule 16 violations, in order to affirm a trial verdict by simply not addressing the claims made by the accused in motions or appeals? Does the intentional avoidance of accused claims create a violation of his due process rights? H.
