Duane Jackson v. United States

IFP petition · United States Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit, No. 24-3293 · judgment July 29, 2026

Pending

Question presented

Following this Court’s holding in N.Y. State Rifle & Pistol Ass’n v. Bruen, 597 U.S. 1 (2022), courts must engage in a history-based analysis when deciding whether a firearm law is part of the historical tradition that delimits the outer boundaries of the right to keep and bear arms. To do so, a court must determine whether the Second Amendment’s plain text covers a person’s conduct; and, if so, whether the Government has demonstrated the law is consistent with the Nation’s historical tradition of firearm regulation. Here, the Third Circuit circumvented the Bruen framework in addressing Petitioner’s as-applied challenge to his lifetime firearm ban imposed under 18 U.S.C. §922(g)(1), by focusing on his unrelated, temporary status as a probationer and supervised releasee. The question presented is: Whether a court may avoid analyzing an as-applied challenge to §922(g)(1)’s lifetime ban by looking, instead, at whether the individual is under some form of temporary supervision in the community?
