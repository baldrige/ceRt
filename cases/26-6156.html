Chicoby Summers v. United States

IFP petition · United States Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit, No. 25-5006, 25-5007 · judgment June 24, 2026

Pending

Question presented

Under 18 U.S.C. § 924(c)(1)(A), a defendant can be convicted of using or carrying a firearm “during and in relation to” a drug crime, or possessing a firearm “in furtherance of” one. The government charged Mr. Summers with the “in furtherance” prong of the statute. At trial, the evidence showed that Mr. Summers possessed a firearm on three days while outside a single residence, where—on different days—narcotics and paraphernalia were recovered. The government contended Mr. Summers’ possession was “in furtherance” of a drug crime based on expert testimony that drug dealers rely on guns to protect their drug activity. The Sixth Circuit affirmed the conviction based on its precedent that “in furtherance” is satisfied by showing that a defendant possessed a firearm for protection during a drug conspiracy. This petition presents the following question: I. Is a gun’s general purpose of “protection” sufficient to establish that its possession is “in furtherance” of a drug crime under 18 U.S.C. § 924(c)(1)(A) when the government offers no evidence that such possession coincides in time, space, and purpose with a drug-related act?
