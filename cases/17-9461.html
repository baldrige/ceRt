Danica Bulovic v. The Stop & Shop Supermarket Company, LLC, et al.

IFP petition · United States Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit, No. 16-3341 · judgment October 5, 2017

Certiorari denied · October 1, 2018

Question presented

Question is: Whether United Food & Commercial Workers Internatonal Union Industry Pension Fund Future Service Pension Plan ("U.F.C.W. International Union Plan") violate: Section 411 (d)(6) unti-cutback Rule of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986, as amended ("the Code"), 204 (g) of The Employee Retirement Income Security Act of 1974,("ERISA") as amended, and Section 4022(a) of Title IV of ERISA a Plan Termination Insurance Program "non- forfeitable" "individual accrued benefits determined under the plan.Sec. 1.2 of the Plan, (1002(23)(A), 29 U.S.C. 1054(c)(3), if U.F.C.W. International Union Plan permits U.F.C.W Local 1500 International Union Administrator discretion to provide or not provide particular form or type of protected benefits, under the Pension Plan, at Normal Plan's retirement date 60, which Petitioner reached in February 1997 when Private Employer Royal Ahold closed all it's Supermarkets in Westchester County, N.Y. Qualified plan is maintained by International Union under Collective Bargaining Agreements and section 1001 as part of Multiemployer Pension Plan Amendments Act of 1980, which Plan Petitioner discovered and filed application for overdue benefit in October 2013. "The term 'nonforfeitable' means a claim.., to that part of a... benefit. which arises from participant's service, which is . . unconditional. and which is legally enforceable against the plan 29 U.S.C. 1002(19),insured by Pension Benefit Guaranty Corporation. The following facts are undisputed: On May 1, 1995 private employer Royal Ahold, known as Stop & Shop pursuant to a collective bargained Agreements with First National Supermarket merged into multi employer defined plan covering employees represented by U.F.C.WLocal 1500 International Union. Benefits accrued under a merged participating Plan are subject to the provisions of that Plan immediately before participation in the Plan. Payments of benefits are blocked, based on previous Judgment for statutory violations for breaches of fiduciary duty for failure to provide plan documents under sec.502. In Duffy v, Brannen ,529 A 2d 643,Vt.1987 the court held that her claim for statutory violation is not within the scope of state court jurisdiction. If Petitioner in the original action for pension plan documents had not standing to sue under principles governing the federal court see ASARCO Inc. v. Kadish, 490 U.S. 605 (1989), See also Marriage of Oddino, 939 P.2d 1266 (Cal. 1997). Supreme Court may grant the petition on certiorari, because "ERISA preempts any and all State Laws in so far as they may now or hereafter relate to any employee benefit plan" 29 U.S.C. 1144.
