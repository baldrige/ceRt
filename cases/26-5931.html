Aurelio Quintanilla, Jr. v. United States

IFP petition · United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit, No. 25-20230 · judgment July 7, 2026

Pending

Questions presented

In Hunter v. United States, 608 U.S. 713 (2026), this Court held that an appeal waiver “is unenforceable when it would result in a miscarriage of justice,” rejected the Fifth Circuit’s rule that a valid waiver yields in “only two” circumstances, and remanded because the Fifth Circuit “did not address” the miscarriage-of-justice question. Nineteen days later, in this case, the Fifth Circuit enforced a waiver in the same form on the sole ground that it “is valid and encompasses” petitioner’s sentencing claims. It did not mention Hunter. It then denied rehearing that raised Hunter squarely. The panel also enforced the waiver against petitioner’s challenge to a $17,500 restitution order under 18 U.S.C. § 2259, a claim the Government had conceded was “not barred by the appellate waiver.” It did so by holding that the district court had “conducted the required inquiry.” Section 2259(b)(2) directs the court to “determine the full amount of the victim’s losses” and then to order restitution reflecting “the defendant’s relative role in the causal process.” The district court stated no losses, made no finding on causation or apportionment, adopted the amounts the victims requested, and left the judgment’s “Total Loss” column blank. The questions presented are:

  1. Whether a court of appeals may enforce an appeal waiver against a challenge to a sentence without deciding whether enforcement would result in a miscarriage of justice, as Hunter requires.

  2. Whether a court of appeals may enforce an appeal waiver against a claim that the Government expressly conceded the waiver does not bar.

  3. Whether a district court satisfies 18 U.S.C. § 2259(b)(2) and Paroline v. United States, 572 U.S. 434 (2014), by adopting the restitution amount a victim requests without determining the victim’s losses or the defendant’s relative causal role.
