Carlouse Latour Allbrooks v. North Carolina

IFP petition · Court of Appeals of North Carolina, No. COA16-741 · judgment November 21, 2017

Certiorari denied · October 1, 2018

Question presented

In Blueford v. Arizona, 566 U.S. 599, 132 S.Ct. 2044, 182 L.Ed. 2d 937 (2012), Justice Sotomayor wrote the following; “A jury’s genuine inability to reach a verdict constitutes manifest necessity.” Id at 619. With the utmost respect, Petitioner contends that such a statement is tautological. Petitioner requests review to address the critical issues of when “first jeopardy” attaches, and whether the failure of a jury to reach a verdict automatically relieves the State of its burden to prove the existence of a manifest necessity to discharge the jury before retrying the Petitioner following a hung jury. Petitioner believes that his second trial is inconsistent with the fundamental distinctions between the European Inquisitorial system of criminal jurisprudence and the American Adversary system. Petitioner’s case provides an excellent vehicle to address the issue. The Question Presented is; Whether the State can retry the Petitioner, following a mistrial due to a hung jury, when: A) There was no finding of a manifest necessity made by the trial Judge, B) The Petitioner did not make the motion to declare a mistrial, and C) “First Jeopardy “was not self-imposed by the Petitioner?
