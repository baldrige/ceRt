Barry J. Bluefeld v. Barry S. Cohen, et al.

IFP petition · United States Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit, No. 17-1596 · judgment September 28, 2017

Certiorari denied · April 30, 2018

Question presented

Was the District Court divested of their jurisdiction after each one of the three interlocutory appeals? Did this lack of jurisdiction render subsequent court orders invalid? Did the District Court lack jurisdiction when it ordered the Motion To Dismiss to be granted on April 27, 2017? This was while Appeal 3 was still pending. Did the lack of an impartial trier of the facts cause a denial of Petitioner's 5th Amendment rights of Due process? Will the District Court's order granting the MTD be reversed because of an Abuse of Discretion? What other reason is a contributing factor for remanding the case to the District Court? The Respondents have conducted a fraudulent acquisition of two shopping centers in Maryland by assuming the stolen identity of a businessman in New York with the same last name. Can this be considered a contributing factor to determine whether this case will be taken up for review? Will this help demonstrate the importance of protecting the public by bringing the respondents to justice? These two acquisitions were made under the names of Ravid Mount Airy and Ravid Frederick. An important component of this case is how Barry Cohen has defrauded the State of Maryland by evading payment of his State Page 3 of 56 personal income taxes. This has occurred over many years and has probably cost the state over $2M in lost tax revenue and statutory penalties and interest. Could this be thought of as an indication of how the citizens of Maryland have been injured and the importance of remanding this case for the public good? Both criminal offenses (tax evasion in MD and fraudulent acquisition of two shopping centers) involve identity theft. With the shopping centers, this activity shows a high level of bank and mortgage fraud. By a restrictive supervision of discovery, the court has prevented the Petitioner from adding on the cause of action of "money laundering". Since this would be a simple matter to prove in court and would bring much needed funds for the public welfare, the respondents should be made to answer to this federal crime and pay back federal income taxes. Does this favorable treatment illustrate a bias toward the respondents and an abuse of discretion by the District Court? Shouldn't this cause of action be allowed to be incorporated into a second Amended Complaint? If the respondents are forced to pay back the IRS for the back taxes that were lost due to "money laundering", remanding this case back to the District Court would bring a high return and relief with the funds that would be paid back. This would surely be for the public good. Why would a court not want to add this important cause of action against the Page 4 of 56 Defendants? That would be a disregard of the country's fiscal needs. Would the reparation of these back taxes to the federal government be ample justification for the extra expense of a full trial (if necessary)? And also to justify the expense of appointing an attorney for the Petitioner? If the respondent's money laundering activities were stopped, would this not send a message to other money laundering offenders throughout the country that this important statute will be enforced? If something were done to curtail this prevalent financial crime, would it not help the new tax system that was just instituted, to succeed? Since David Cohen often states that he is employed as the Chief Counsel of Meisel & Cohen Properties, he should be aware of all the racketeering activities of his father- Barry Cohen and Barry's partnerJoel Meisel, He has participated as a legal counsel in all these criminal offenses. Would the Respondents agree to cooperate and settle this dispute in a fair manner, so that David Cohen's legal malfeasance does not have to be reported to the Attorney Grievance Commission of Maryland? Was the "taking" of Petitioner's $4,110.64 in June 2012 a violation of the RICO Statute 1964 (c) by creating an illegal debt? If this was a RICO violation directed at the Petitioner, does this enable him to Pages of 56 receive Treble damages, as the statute requires? This injury was inflicted only on Petitioner and no one else nor the corporation. Does this case comply with the requirements set forth in the RICO Statute and qualify as a valid RICO suit? It appears to match up on several levels with Justice White's majority opinion written on Sedima in 1985? This controversy was thought to be settled by the first JudgeJudge Hazel, but it was reversed by the second Judge- Judge Xinis. How is it possible that the District Court of Maryland, under two different judges- George Hazel and Paula Xinis, have two very different decisions on the same RICO amended complaint? Judge Xinis was assigned to this case immediately after her Senate confirmation and has gone out of her way to take it apart. She has considered this case to be unworthy of RICO litigation. Her predecessor- Judge Hazel, followed normal procedures and allowed an Amended Complaint with all the prerequisites of a RICO lawsuit. How can the same District Court have two vastly different views on whether this case qualifies as RICO? This is an important reason for the Supreme Court to settle this question and the difference of opinion in the same district court. If the District Court did not have jurisdiction over this case during the timeframe of the appeals, why was the Petitioner required to have an attorney representing him before that court?
