Darryl Schneider v. Robert Welker

IFP petition · District Court of Appeal of Florida, Second District, No. 2D17-2243 · judgment March 23, 2018

Certiorari denied · October 1, 2018

Question presented

: Is it not a national concern and the responsibility of the federal government to protect its elderly citizens’ property from being stolen from them using a loophole in the probate statutes and common laws which allows anyone to retain an attorney in drafting inheritance contracts for someone else’s estate plans, thus permitting future theft of property from the sick and feeble minded with just their signature? Is it the responsibility of all U.S. courts of law to ask that law enforcement investigate a suspected murder and not help in covering one up, no matter who is involved, if probable cause (by the preponderance of evidence) has been established? Should it be the responsibility of all U.S. court of law to toll indefinitely any civil claims for damages as a result of a suspected murder until a murder investigation has been completed by law enforcement, if the results could have an impact on the jury’s verdict for civil damages caused by the murder? Is the state of Florida’s judicial system required to use the third-party beneficiary of contract rule, not the Florida/Iowa beneficiary rule, in deciding if a party to a probate case had privity with a negligent attorney for tort lawsuit purposes, if the attorney was not a party to, or in privity with any party in, the probate case? Did the Florida trial court judge, the Second Florida District Court of Appeals and the Florida Supreme Court all violate both the decedent’s and the Petitioner’s substantive due process rights and liberties in cases 16-CA-4693 and 2D17-2243, on pretextual, arbitrary and capricious reasons when they either cited Florida Statute -- -- 733.103, or stated that a 2DCA opinion was needed to review the case with? The counts in case 16-CA-4693 were for legal malpractice, fraud and gross negligence in a circuit civil, tort lawsuit against a non-party attorney, not for undue influence and competency issues in a probate lawsuit against the personal representative (PR) and beneficiaries for probated estate property to have cited Florida Statute 733.103. And a per curiam opinion by an appellate court is an opinion which carries no less weight because of the nomenclature that designates it as such. See Foley v. Weaver Drugs, Inc., Supreme Court of Florida, No. 32357, (1965). Did Congress intend for substantive due process rights to be bestowed to all of its citizens and honored by all U.S. courts of law under the 14th Amendment to the U.S. Constitution, or are these rights only for citizens who have not been prejudiced by elected officials for increased satisfaction, profit and gain? Would it not be a much more legitimate, accurate and extremely efficient process of correctly and consistently ruling on court cases, if computers and magistrates replaced lower court judges before trial? This way any judicial computer used would give parties in a case the same impartial and correct rulings up until the need for judges to preside. Why have legislative branches of government revising the statutes for a particular purpose, if judges are allowed to apply any irrelevant statute arbitrarily to suit their means? Is an amendment to a state’s constitution that make it mandatory for the state supreme court not to oversee the state appellate courts correctly reviewing final iv -- --- - -- -- -- - 4 orders by keeping the applicable laws harmonious and uniformly applied, a violation of equal rights under the 14th Amemdment to the U.S. Constitution? When the Florida Supreme Court, in Wells v. State, 132 So.3d 1110 (Fla. 2014), changed its constitutional requirements, limiting its review under Article V, Section 3(b)3 to just elaborated per curiam decisions, it refused to stop substantive due process rights from being upheld at the appellate court level, a violation of under 42 U.S.C.A. § 1983 and 42 U.S.C.A. § 1988 (1976)? Parties to Proceedings The parties to this court case are Darryl Schneider, Petitioner and Robert Welker, Respondent, currently represented by the CNA Insurance Company.
