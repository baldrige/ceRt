Joanne Stone v. Louisiana Department of Revenue

IFP petition · United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit, No. 16-30843 · judgment August 31, 2017

Certiorari denied · May 14, 2018

Question presented

Issues Can this Supreme Court exercise its original jurisdiction in this case because the State is a party to the lawsuit? The case was actually heard by Magistrate Judge Roby at pre-trial hearings and decisions without parties granting consent on some issues. Petitioner had no attorney in early stages of the case and consented to the Magistrate Judge’s jurisdiction, but no consent was given to take away a right to a jury trial (as requested). Did Petitioner engage in a protected activity when she complained to her directors about discriminatory harassment (counting the complaint as likened to filing a grievance) leading to retaliation? Can the courts ignore the (discriminatory harassment complaint) which is the link required for the new EEOC 3-prong Test that led to the retaliation in violation of equal protection under the law? More importantly, can this new EEOC 3-Prong test replace McDonnell Douglas -burden shifting case established by the U.S. Supreme Court & Price Waterhouse? Was there a violation of the anti-discrimination laws? The different ways that discrimination can occur shows the complaint was for discrimination and the resignation itself is part of the discrimination. Both direct and indirect evidence exist (as first held by the Appeals Court original 3 judges but then three different judges ruled differently upon appeal). Now, an unreasonable result was reached. Does a complaint of harassment/discrimination and retaliation (count the same as a formal charge filing with the EEOC) to establish a link between the complained about action and the retaliation? Originally, Appeals court correctly held retaliation was established and used May 20, 2017 complaint date. Upon re hearing, the Appeals court arbitrarily and capriciously decides to use a different method (new EEOC 3 Prong Test (causality prong) to conclude Stone’s retaliation argument. This Court’s case precedent of McDonnell Douglas (a reasonable basis) was ignored. McDonnel shifts the burden on to the Defendant. Respondent failed to show evidence of his position per McDonnel case. The Appeals Court 2017 ruling states” district court noted the omission of Lockley’s deposition in the record before it, and explaining that the record was therefore “unclear as to whether Lockley had knowledge that Stone engaged in the protected activity.” The record evidences both that complaints were made, and that Stone was transferred. Therefore, as the burden shifts per the 3 of 40 McDonnell Douglas Test, It is the Defendant that had no evidence, Stone’s retaliation arguments must prevail. Did defamation exist? Petitioner struggles everyday with this. Trying to get back in society. Did Constitutional violations exist? Was a right of trial by jury preserved and equal protection under the law granted or arbitrary acts made without regard for the law, and First Amendment right to petition the government violated? Magistrate Judge sought to mislead petitioner about right to jury trial. On 12/20/2012 Document 1, the Complaint with Jury demand & on 9/12/13 Document 26 Amended Complaint with Jury demand (specifically state and clearly evidences a (Jury Demand). On 10/21/2013, (only 39 days later) it is only reasonable to known that Petitioner did not have any convincing reason to change her mind. Petitioner was without attorney when two things happened 1. Petitioner was present and a Jury Trial was set for 6/30/2014 08:30 AM before Magistrate Judge Karen Wells Roby and 2. Petitioner on this same date was presented with document 32 of what Petitioner understood to mean that Judge Roby “as a judge” the judge would be over the trial proceedings (not that there would be “no jury”). Petitioner did not understand “no jury part” as the document is absent on that matter, in fact the word “jury” does not appear anywhere on the form. Petitioner was not adequately advised about matters and was harmed. Petitioner had no attorney. Due process clause right’s to life, liberty, and property without proper notice and the opportunity to be heard was violated when convincing evidence exists and is part of R.O.A. Stone was deceived by Judges spoken word that a jury trial would take place and that she was not signing away any rights to a jury trial. Did harmful improper actions and severe errors occur by lower courts, as other courts handled issues differently? Review why case dismissed on summary judgment as initial appeal court review held that there was prima facie evidence for a jury trial (as requested in complaint). The appeals court reversed itself with change in EEOC laws, resulting in conflicting rulings within its own appeals court, the district trial courts, and U.S. Supreme Court. Three conflicts: 1). Conflict on application of new EEOC 3-Prong Test for prima facie. 2) Conflicts on rulings about timeliness of the 2013 EEOC charge: a) A conflicting position held between district and appeals court and within the appeals court itself. b) 2013 charge deemed timely by Appeals Court 2017 Rehearing. It included disparate treatment & constructive discharge as it was reported while EEOC 2010 charge open and matters were effectively exhausted. Stone made several requests about 4 of 40 disparate treatment (laws require you to go above violator & telling the party to the complaint again is telling him what he already knows)-reasonableness rules here. 3) Retaliation was found to have survived, per original Appeals ruling, why now changed when court held that there was evidence? McDonnell Douglas v. Green and Texas Dept. of Community Affairs v. Burdine has been used as determination of prima facie case not the new EEOC three prongs & also Price Waterhouse case. Can courts leave out issues briefed and not considered new matters briefed e.g. constructive discharge ignored & disparate treatment? Unreasonable for appellant’s motion for leave to file petition for rehearing out of time is DENIED when reasonable request made for time for matters within motion for withdrawal of attorney was approved. The Circuit court admits that many errors were made at district court level in footnote “2” of its ruling it is stated “district court seems to have erroneously applied this circuit’s previous standard for adverse employment action in retaliation suits...” Answers Original jurisdiction per the U.S. Constitution is exercised: “According to Article Ill. Section 2. The judicial Power shall extend to all Cases in Law and Equity, arising under this Constitution, the Laws of the United States and Treaties made, or which shall be made, under their Authority;-to all Cases affecting Ambassadors, other public Ministers and Consuls, and those in which a State shall be Party, the Supreme Court shall have original Jurisdiction. In all the other Cases before mentioned, the Supreme Court shall have appellate Jurisdiction, both as to Law and Fact, with such Exceptions, and under such Regulations as the Congress shall make.” Plaintiff Stone engage in a protected activity when she complained. Stone complained to managers and directors about her employment issues and filed a grievance and an EEOC charge. The complaints were done with supervisory personnel well before a formal grievance was filed. Plaintiff also wrote letters and sent emaits upset over the handling of out-of-state positions which then led to the resulting continuous retaliatory discrimination and eventual constructive discharge. In other cases, a complaint of harassment was considered as engaging in protected activity. Director Kurt Vanbroklin answered with bias in the choice of remedy it provided and it was not until July 2010 that plans for transfer would be put in place for Stone to leave the State. The investigation was rigged to reach a pre-determined conclusion or otherwise conducted in bad faith and did not satisfy the remedial obligation. Stone was essentially removed from the out-of-state work from home location in Mobile, AL that she sought since April 2008 onward. See Swenson v. Potter, 271 F.3d 1184 (gth Cir. 2001). Inadequate response Inadequate investigation and responses to issues, multiple searches conducted at Petitioner on the job were and ended telecommute were to embarrass and drive the plaintiff out of the company and is enough to fulfill ‘malice or reckless disregard’ standard, justifying the award of punitive damages to the plaintiff.
