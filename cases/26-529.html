Craig R. Becker v. United States

Question presented

In Chambers v. Mississippi, 410 U.S. 284 (1973) and Holmes v. South Carolina, 547 U.S. 319 (2006), this Court reinforced the Sixth Amendment’s guarantee of the right to present a complete defense, which necessarily includes evidence of third-party liability in murder cases. Petitioner stands convicted of murdering his then-wife. His main defense at trial was the theory that she committed suicide. But the military trial court excluded material and relevant evidence, later determined not to be hearsay, that his wife was “terrified” of losing her “memory” and “mental faculties.” The Navy-Marine Corps Court of Criminal Appeals (NMCCA) acknowledged both the hearsay ruling and Mil. R. Evid. 403 rulings were erroneous, but nevertheless affirmed, finding the evidence lacked relevance or probative value. The Court of Appeals for the Armed Forces (CAAF) declined review. The question presented is: Whether the trial court’s exclusion of the decedent’s statements about her mental health violated Petitioner’s constitutional right to present a complete defense.
