Munson P. Hunter, III v. United States

IFP petition · United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit, No. 24-20211 · judgment July 29, 2026

Pending

Questions presented

In Hunter v. United States, 608 U.S. 713 (2026), this Court held that an appeal waiver in a plea agreement is unenforceable when it would result in a “miscarriage of justice,” which would occur when leaving in place the kind of “obvious” and “egregious” error “that would bring the judicial system into disrepute.” Id. at 724. This Court remanded for the Fifth Circuit to decide whether enforcing petitioner’s appeal waiver – which would leave intact a special condition of supervised release requiring him to take any psychotropic medication that a physician prescribes, over his objection and without any particularized, medically-grounded findings by the sentencing court – would constitute such a miscarriage of justice. The Fifth Circuit held that it would not, concluding that neither its precedent nor this Court’s precedent establishes the constitutional error alleged by petitioner as an “obvious” error worthy of correcting notwithstanding the appellate waiver. The questions presented are:

  1. Whether, under this Court’s decision in Hunter, a sentencing error constitutes an “obvious” error rendering an appeal waiver potentially unenforceable as a “miscarriage of justice” only if controlling precedent of this Court or the court of appeals already has resolved the precise question.

  2. Whether, under this Court’s decision in Hunter, a district court’s supervised release condition requiring a mentally competent, non-dangerous defendant to take psychotropic medication prescribed by a medical doctor – imposed over the defendant’s objection and without particularized, medically-grounded findings – is an “egregious” constitutional error that, if left uncorrected on appeal, would bring the judicial system into disrepute.
