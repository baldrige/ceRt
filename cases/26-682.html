Questions presented

Petitioner Cyrus Sanai’s lawsuit attacking California’s attorney discipline scheme was dismissed on Younger abstention grounds. This Court denied his petition for certiorari, Case No. 21-15771 and a month later issued Williams v. Reed, 604 U.S. 168 (2025), which held that under Haywood v. Drown, 556 U.S. 729 (2009), administrative hearing schemes that due to state-law jurisdiction stripping cannot be attacked under 42 U.S.C. §1983 in state court violate the Supremacy Clause. California’s State Bar Act strips its courts of general jurisdiction from hearing §1983 lawsuit concerning attorney discipline, Ninth Circuit precedent holds that Younger abstention does not bar attacks on state administrative procedures that violate the Supremacy Clause. Potrero Hills Landfill, Inc. v. County of Solano, 657 F.3d 876, 884 fn. 9 (9th Cir. 2011). The Ninth Circuit refused to apply Haywood and Williams because under Miller v. Gammie, 335 F.3d 889 (9th Cir. 2003)(en banc) a regular panel cannot change prior Ninth Circuit precedent unless this Court’s new decision “clearly irreconcilable” and not “merely inconsistent.” Whitfield v. Mullin, Case No. 25-5220 (9th Cir. Oct. 16, 2026)(VanDyke, J, diss.). The courts in the Ninth Circuit “systematically hold the Supreme Court’s instructions about the right answer to any number of legal questions in lower regard than” the Ninth Circuit’s “own wrong precedents.” Id., slip op. at 14 (emphasis in original). The questions presented are:

  1. Is the Ninth Circuit’s rule for amending its circuit precedents established by Miller v. Gammie, 335 F.3d 889 (9th Cir. 2003) as supplemented by Kivett v. Flagstar Bank, 154 F.4th 640 (9th Cir. 2025), petition pending sub nom. Flagstar Bank v. Kivett, Case No. 25-1350, which does not permit changing circuit precedent unless the Ninth Circuit decides the issue en banc or if a three-judge panel finds this Court’s precedent “clearly irreconcilable” a violation of equal protection under the Fifth Amendment and of the principles of stare decisis?

  2. If a state prohibits its courts of general jurisdiction from hearing 42 U.S.C. §1983 claims addressing attorney discipline proceedings, do such proceedings and the state law establishing them violate the Supremacy Clause under Haywood v. Drown, 556 U.S. 729 (2009) and Williams v Reed, 604 U.S. 168 (2025)as agreed by the Washington State Court of Appeals in Eugster v. Wash. State Bar Assoc.,198 Wash.App. 758, 397 P.3d 131, 143-6 (2017) and therefore Younger abstention does not apply under Potrero Hills Landfill, Inc. v. County of Solano, 657 F.3d 876, 884 fn. 9 (9th Cir. 2011) and cases cited therein?

  3. Does the deferral of Sanai’s motion for preliminary relief violate the guarantees of due process and equal protection, as well as Nken v. Holder, 556 U.S. 418 (2009), particularly as some appeals are given automatic relief under Ninth Circuit General Order 6.4(c) and then deferred to the merits panel, effectively operating as an automatic stay, while the deferral in the case of other litigants operates as an automatic denial of preliminary relief?

  4. Given that the California State Bar is a California public corporation the debts of which are not the responsibility of the State of California, can it no longer be deemed an “arm of the state” under Galette v. New Jersey Transport Corp., 607 U.S. 509, 146 S.Ct. 854 (2026) and thus not be protected by Younger abstention under Sprint Commc’ns, Inc. v. Jacobs, 571 U.S. 69, 79 (2013) and New Orleans Public Service, Inc. v. Council of City of New Orleans, 491 U.S. 350 (1989)?
