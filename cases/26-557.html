Dennis Gail Tilton v. City of Eastlake, Ohio

Paid petition · Court of Appeals of Ohio, Lake County, No. 2025-L-016, 2025-L-017 · judgment December 8, 2025

Questions presented

  1. Whether a state appellate court may reject a claim of suppressed evidence on the ground that counsel’s failure to use that evidence was “trial strategy,” and that “[f]or all we know” the evidence “w[as] available to appellant during discovery,” where the defendant has told that court the opposite and the court has declined to employ the record-correction procedures available to it — and where the State played a booking recording to the jury, told the jury on the record that “[t]he jury has the video,” and furnished the defendant, for his appeal, one minute and fortyfour seconds of it.

  2. Whether the Due Process Clause permits a conviction to rest on a stipulation to an element of the offense — here, the prior conviction that converts R.C. 4511.19(A)(2) into the count carrying the entire sentence — where the stipulation was signed outside the courtroom, the trial court stated on the record that it had never seen the document, no colloquy established the defendant’s knowing and voluntary assent, the jury was instructed “[t]he parties have stipulated to this element of the offense; therefore, you are to accept this element as true,” and the state appellate court held that the defendant’s claim of duress “can only be raised in a petition for post­ conviction relief’ that no Ohio court will entertain.

  3. Whether Webb v. Texas, 409 U.S. 95 (1972), is violated where a trial court, during a recess in the testimony of the defendant’s sole corroborating witness and after striking her central answer, tells her she “will not be permitted to continue with [her] testimony” and that the court’s enforcement mechanisms “include [] a fine, a finding of contempt of court, and you can be jailed up to 30 days,” after which defense counsel states he “will just ask a few questions,” the examination ends within two pages, and no redirect follows — and whether Webb reaches a witness whose testimony is curtailed rather than prevented.

  4. Whether a State may deny all review of a federal claim by directing it to a forum that does not exist, where the court of appeals held the claim could “only be raised in a petition for post-conviction relief,” the municipal court that imposed sentence held it has no jurisdiction over such petitions, the court of common pleas held it is not the sentencing court, and the court of appeals then held that no court had jurisdiction at all. u
