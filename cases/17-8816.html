Gregory T. Ackerman, et ux. v. The Bank of New York Mellon

IFP petition · Supreme Court of Ohio, No. 2017-0385 · judgment May 31, 2017

Certiorari denied · October 1, 2018

Question presented

Why has the Constitution of the United States and Rule of Law failed the citizens of the United States of America upon a legal party’s “Jury Demand” action in a court of law? Are the State of Ohio Government and United States Government bound by the law, to treat all people with respect and equality in matters of justice, and foremost recognize American citizens’ fundamental, substantive and substantial rights in a court of law, specifically when the Administrative Record dictates :a paramount “Jury Demand” action to be enforced for the past 17+ years while case matters are under a “void judgments”? Pursuant to 28 U.S. Code § 1257 State Courts; certiorari, 42 U.S. Code § 1983 Civil action for deprivation of rights, and 28 U.S. Code § 1657 Priority of civil actions) upon - “terminated” the State of Ohio; Montgomery County Common Pleas Court Case No. 2000CV1472, and independent case No. 2003 CV9499; to a paramount “Jury Demand” action, as preserved and inviolate to their “ultimate fundamental right(s)” in their State and Federal (Dual tracking) constitutional right in action to a “trial by jury”; to determine all genuine legal issues of disputed material facts for a jury to decide, not an administrative judge, upon case matters in the “business of insurance”, and State of Ohio; Montgomery County Common Pleas Court Case No. 2009CV03 194 How will the Supreme Court Stay the Respondent’s foreclosure proceedings while the Petitioners pursue and correct a “deprivation of rights under the Constitution of the United States”?
