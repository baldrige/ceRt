Question presented

In a civil case, an appellate court must disregard any error that was harmless and, as this Court explained in Shinseki v. Sanders, 556 U.S. 396 (2009), the party challenging the judgment bears the burden of showing harm. But when it comes to an error in the jury instructions, the Ninth Circuit places the burden on a civil appellee defending the judgment below to prove that the error was harmless. Every other circuit to address the question places the burden on the appellant challenging the judgment to prove that the error was not harmless, as Shinseki and several other decisions of this Court dictate. The question presented is: Whether there is an exception for erroneous jury instructions to the otherwise uniform rule that the appellant bears the burden in a civil case to establish that an error was prejudicial.
