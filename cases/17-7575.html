Katherine B. Robinson, et al. v. Chesapeake Bank of Maryland, et al.

IFP petition · United States Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit, No. 17-1796 · judgment November 27, 2017

Certiorari denied · April 2, 2018

Question presented

Why did Chesapeake Bank promise to pay Petitioners for the 1983 fire damages when the premiums were brought current but refused to pay Petitioners when the mortgage was brought current and even paid off? Why did the Honorable Judge Brown rule the March 25, 2011, thunder storm incident that collapsed the rear of Petitioners' Property that contributed to the selling of Petitioners' insured Property as passed the three (3) year Statute of Limitation along with the 1983 fire that occurred at the rear of the dwelling not the front as Proctor claims was partly the cause of the damages to the front porch, and the 1996 snow blizzard and the 2007 continuous water leak? Why did the Honorable Judge Brown omit hearing the March 25, 2011, incident that was filed January 31, 2014, which was not passed the three (3) year statute of limitation the incident that caused the collapsing of the rear of Petitioners' insured Property that contributed to the selling of Petitioners' insured Property on February 18, 2014? On November 10, 2014, Appellee requested a Motion Hearing on Case No.: 24-C-13-008544, Katherine B. Robinson vs. Chesapeake Bank of Maryland, et al, (hereafter Chesapeake). Out of the three defenses presented by Defendant, the Statute of Limitations was the only defense heard so how could the March 25, 2011 incident filed on January 31, 2014 be passed the Statute of Limitation? When the Honorable Judge Brown asked Mr. C. William Clark, attorney for Chesapeake, was it his argument that the Statute of Limitations argument was the dispositive of the entire matter? Mr. Clark answered yes. The fire of 1983, the snow blizzard of 1996 and the 2007 water damages were the only incidents that were heard in the hearing that were passed the Statute of Limitation. The March 25, 2011 incident was not heard, and not mentioned so how could it be considered pass the Statute of Limitations? Did the Honorable Judge Brown erred when he GRANTED Defendant Chesapeake's Motion for Summary Judgment based on the expiration of the three (3) year Statute of Limitations including the March 25, 2011 incident in that ORDER? Did the Honorable Judge Brown erred in denying Petitioners' March 25, 2011 incident (the storm that collapsed the rear of the property) that was filed in a timely manner (Case No.: 24-C-13-008544/OT) not to be heard that lead to the selling of Petitioners' insured property? Is it the blame of the Petitioners that the November 10, 2014 ORDER did not come to Petitioners' home as advised by the Honorable Judge Brown due to a new mail carrier? Is it the blame of the Petitioners that the Petitioners had to purchase the Honorable Judge Brown's ORDER from the Circuit Court's file room and then filed the Appeal which was late filing?
