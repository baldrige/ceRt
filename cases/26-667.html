Questions presented

Petitioner permanently lost all use of her left arm when a law enforcement officer shot her with an explosive munition while she was unarmed and retreating. She brought a Section 1983 lawsuit against that officer under the Fourth and Fourteenth Amendments, alleging that he violated her civil rights by using excessive force. The Eighth Circuit affirmed dismissal of Petitioner’s Fourth Amendment claim, holding that no seizure had occurred because, in the Court’s view, the officer’s goal was to disperse, rather than to detain, her. The Eighth Circuit held that Petitioner’s claim under the Fourteenth Amendment failed because the officer’s conduct did not “shock the conscience.” The questions presented are:

  1. Whether the standard for Fourteenth Amendment excessive force claims by unseized civilians is “shocks the conscience,” as the Eighth and Ninth Circuits have held, or the “objective reasonableness” standard that applies to pretrial detainees under Kingsley v. Hendrickson, 576 U.S. 389 (2015), as the Second and Eleventh Circuits have held.

  2. Whether a crowd-control device intentionally deployed at a civilian always constitutes a seizure when it stops the civilian’s movement, as the Second, Ninth, and Tenth Circuits have held, or only when the officer’s intent was to detain the civilian, as the Eighth and Sixth Circuits have held.
