Robert L. Hamilton v. Don Strahota, Warden

IFP petition · United States Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit, No. 16-3859 · judgment May 3, 2017

Certiorari denied · October 1, 2018

Question presented

is one of national Importance. This case presents an important constitutional issue that is likely to recur in many cases throughout the country! II. Consequently the issue in this case will continue to recur as, legally speaking all such case have one thing in common: Ill. This case is an excellent vehicle for resolution of this important issue. In the order denying motion for post-conviction relief dated May 28 2013 page 7 “Because the motion had not been filed at the time of the plea hearing the court spoke to the defendant in terms of the possibility of a successful motion.” Pleas are a waiver of constitutional rights there not subject to a possibility a defendant must know what the punishment is before taking a plea the court cannot make a defendant wait until the day of sentencing to determine what the punishment will be. Defense counsel estimated that dismissing four out of the five habitual enhancers would decrease the total maximum sentence by 28 to 35 years. (125: 6-7; App. 117-118). Defense counsel did not state on which counts the penalty enhancers would be dismissed. The Court instructed defense counsel to submit briefing prior to sentencing (125: 7-8; App. During the plea colloquy, the Circuit court advised Mr. Hamilton of the maximum penalty on each offense, but stated that the maximum penalty on each offense “could be less later on.” CONCLUSION The U.S. Court should hear this case because when a defendant takes a plea he/she must know the punishment in this case the punishment has always been in question Respectfully Submitted I. j/c1d2’ Dated this July 312017
