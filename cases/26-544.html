In Re Joseph A. Cammarata

Questions presented

  1. Whether this Court should issue the writ where a citizen has been imprisoned for nearly five years, and stripped of more than $100 million, for conduct this Court unanimously held lawful in Sprint Communications Co. v. APCC Services, Inc., 554 U.S. 269 (2008), such that to punish him for doing what the law plainly allows is “a due process violation of the most basic sort,” Bordenkircher v. Hayes, 434 U.S. 357, 363 (1978); where every forum that could correct the error has refused to reach it, a statutorily disqualified district judge with a documented financial interest in the outcome, a structurally conflicted court of appeals whose Executive Office employs the prosecuting attorney’s spouse and which has neither compelled any responsive brief nor ruled in more than thirty-one months, and a prosecution that has never once contested the facts; and whether, to protect “the fairness, integrity, [and] public reputation of judicial proceedings,” United States u. Olano, 507 U.S. 725, 736 (1993), the writ must issue where no other forum remains to review a documented and uncontested miscarriage of justice.

  2. Whether this Court should exercise its supervisory power where the Department of Justice prosecuted, and the Securities and Exchange Commission enforced against, conduct this Court unanimously held lawful in Sprint Communications Co. v. APCC Services, Inc., 554 U.S. 269 (2008); where that controlling authority was suppressed, Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 5(f) was violated, and no court has ever applied it; and where the trial evidence established that Petitioner’s conduct, assigning his own trades to an assignee that filed the class-action settlement claims as the legal beneficial owner, in its own name, and fully disclosed as such, was precisely the conduct Sprint holds lawful.

  3. Whether a writ of mandamus should issue directing reassignment of Third Circuit No. 24-1381 to a conflict-free forum, where the district judge whose orders are on review is disqualified under 28 U.S.C. § 455(b)(4) (financial interest), § 455(b)(5)(i) (party to related litigation against him), and § 455(a) and § 455(b)(1) (documented personal bias and prejudgment), and where a timely and sufficient affidavit of bias under 28 U.S.C. § 144 was denied by the challenged judge himself without referral to another judge as the statute requires.

  4. Whether a writ of mandamus should issue where the Third Circuit is structurally disqualified under 28 U.S.C. § 455(a) by reason of its Executive Office’s employment of the prosecuting Commission attorney’s spouse as an attorney of that office; where that court has never addressed, denied on the merits, or compelled any response to the documented constitutional, due-process, prosecutorial, and judicial-misconduct claims raised across all four related appeals; and by reason of the court’s institutional interest in reviewing its own prior dispositions, and where each of Petitioner’s four appeals, Nos. 23-2110, 24-1983, 25-1188, and this No. 24-1381, has been subject to that same conflicted tribunal, contains uncontested constitutional and prosecutorial violations on the face of its record, and is the subject of proceedings now pending in this Court; and where the Third Circuit’s failure to decide No. 24-1381 for more than thirty-one months, during which the Commission has never filed a merits response brief, has missed every deadline it requested, and has never been ordered to respond, constitutes a constructive denial of appellate review satisfying the “no other adequate means” requirement of Cheney v. United States District Court, 542 U.S. 367, 380-81 (2004).
