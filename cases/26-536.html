Question presented

The Materiality Provision protects voters from being denied the right to vote because of irrelevant paperwork mistakes by prohibiting officials from using an error or omission in an application, registration, or other act requisite to voting that is immaterial to determining if the person meets the State’s voter qualifications. See 52 U.S.C. § 10101(a)(2)(B). In Arkansas, mail voter registration applications must have a handwritten signature or mark (the “wetsignature rule”), which election officials can and do use as a comparison throughout the election process to identify fraud and to determine whether a person is qualified to vote absentee. The Eighth Circuit affirmed a preliminary injunction against enforcement of the wet-signature rule, holding that the Materiality Provision is a demanding standard that gives no deference to States’ chosen electionintegrity measures. In doing so, the Eighth Circuit expressly rejected the Fifth Circuit’s approach to a similar wet-signature rule. The Fifth Circuit held that wet signatures help identify the voter and are therefore material, reasoning that courts must give great weight to a State’s election-integrity judgments. See Vote.org v. Callanen, 89 F.4th 459 (5th Cir. 2023). The question presented is: Does a State violate the Materiality Provision, 52 U.S.C. § 10101(a)(2)(B), by requiring a person’s wet signature on a voter-registration application?
