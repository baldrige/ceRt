Boback Sabeerin v. City of Albuquerque, New Mexico, et al.

IFP petition · Court of Appeals of New Mexico, No. A-1-CA-41777 · judgment February 23, 2026

Pending

Questions presented

  1. Whether a federal district court, exercising federal-question jurisdiction over claims under 42 U.S.C. § 1983, may enter summary judgment dismissing those claims with prejudice on qualified-immunity grounds where the plaintiff alleges and supports with record evidence that officers entered and searched before issuance of a warrant, that the warrant affidavit contained materially false or misleading facts, and that the affiant later made statements arguably undermining the asserted factual nexus to the place searched.

  2. Whether the Tenth Circuit's application of 'arguable probable cause' conflicts with Franks v. Delaware, 438 U.S. 154 (1978), Malley v. Briggs, 475 U.S. 335 (1986), and Groh v. Ramirez, 540 U.S. 551 (2004), when it treated issuance of a warrant and judicial disagreement as supporting immunity without resolving evidence of a pre-warrant search or alleged knowing or reckless material falsehoods.

  3. Whether the Fourteenth Amendment permits a state court to extinguish with prejudice claims arising from an asserted unconstitutional search and seizure without meaningful consideration of the litigant’s continuation, tolling, and dueprocess contentions, where the procedural history was shaped by removal and remand and where the underlying claims had repeatedly been pursued rather than abandoned.

  4. Whether, after defendants remove an action and the federal court remands the remaining state-law claims to the state court from which the action was removed, 28 U.S.C. § 1447(c) permits that state court to resume jurisdiction and decide a state-law venue objection; and, if so, whether removal alone waives that objection.

  5. Whether a state procedural preservation rule constitutes an adequate and independent state ground barring review of a federal claim when, in the exceptional procedural circumstances of this case, the litigant continuously pursued the same controversy through federal dismissal of state claims without prejudice, removal, remand, a venue dismissal without prejudice, refiling in the venue identified as proper, and a later dismissal with prejudice, yet the state appellate court refused to consider the statutory continuation theory because the precise savings-statute argument was not presented below.

  6. Whether Petitioner’s due process rights were preserved when no oral or evidentiary hearing before a state court was not made available to decides a venue motion after the state causes of action had been remanded to the court in Valancia County where the complaint had originally been filed .

  7. Whether a state appellate court may treat a generally applicable preservation rule as an adequate state ground barring consideration of a federal tolling claim when the opposing parties placed the governing tolling issue before the trial court, the case's undisputed procedural history supplied the facts needed to decide it, and rigid application of the rule defeats the federal protection Congress enacted in 28 U.S.C. § 1367(d).

  8. Whether 28 U.S.C. § 1367(d), as construed in Artis v. District of Columbia, 583 U.S. 71 (2018), and Jinks v. Richland County, 538 U.S. 456 (2003), requires a state court deciding timeliness to account for the period during which supplemental state-law claims were pending in federal court before dismissing a refiled action with prejudice as time-barred.
