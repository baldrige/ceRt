David Mark Isaacson v. Lorie Davis, Director, Texas Department of Criminal Justice, Correctional Institutions Division

IFP petition · United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit, No. 16-50565 · judgment August 3, 2017

Certiorari denied · March 19, 2018

Questions presented

  1. Is the Texas Court of Criminal Appeals authorized to dispose of habeas applications in a manner which conflicts with the statutory requirements of the Texas Constitution and Code? According to the.T.C.CA Justice Alcala's analysis in Ex. Parte Dawson, "the Texas Constitution requires a decision by a quorum of judges on this Court, and thus, a denial of habeas relief by a single judge on this. Court fails to comply with this requirement. While it is true that Texas Constitution gives individual judges on this Court the power 'to issue the writ of habeas corpus,' that power does not apply to Article 1107 habeas applications because the Code of Criminal Procedure provides that the issuance of the writ in this context is done by the convicting court rather than by this Court as a whole or it's individual judges." Id, at 2. "Furthermore, the Texas Constitution's mandate that a quorum of judges decide this Court's cases is not satisfied by what is effectively a standing order of this Court that permits an individual judge to act as a proxy for a quorum of judges on this Court on the basis of a pre-vote on a category of cases that are never ac tually individually seen by any judge other than a proxy judge," Justice Alcala continued, .Id at 3. Considering the foregoing, did the T.C.C.A. err in delegating it's decision making responsibility to a proxy judge, in violation of the Texas Constitution and Code of Criminal Procedure?

  2. Did the Court of Criminal Appeals misinterpret the., governing law? Justice Alcala explicitly stated; "Nothing in the Texas Constitution or statutes authorizes a single judge on this Court acting alone to decide habeas applications.. .Thus, a suggestion that a single judge on this Court may deny an Art. 11.07 habeas application because he is merely exercising his constitutional authority to decline to issue the writ is misguided." Id., at 3, 6. "The plain language of Texas Constitution generally gives the judges on this Court the individual power to issue a writ, but that power is subject to regulation by legislature, which has decided no€ to permit the exercise of that power for Art. 11.07 writs. Subsection (c) in section Five of Article five of Texas Constitution discusses the power of each judge on this Court to "issue the writ", which means that the restrainer is commanded to bring the restrained person before the judge. /case/ex-parte-dawson-4#idm139939030882160 Tex. Const. Art. V, § 5(c). That section states: "A judge's decision to issue a writ merely means a command to bring the restrained person before the nudge; it does not mean that relief under the writ is being granted or denied. See Tex. Code Crim. Proc. Art. 11.07." Id., at 4. In light of Justice Alcala's detailed analysis of Tex. Constitution and Code governing disposition of Art. 11.07 habeas applications by a quorum of judges, did the T.C.C.A. misinterpret the law when it held Justice Alcala "had the authority to lawfully deny Dawson's application by her signature alone--because this application satisfied the criterion in the Court's standing internal procedure?" v1].1

  3. Does a single judge on the Court of Criminal Appeals have competentjurisdictiorrto deny Article 11.07 habeas applications? It may be deduced from Justice Alcala's opinion, Texas Legislature has confered competent ,jurisdiction upon a quorum of judges on the Ct. of Crim. App. to decide Art. 11.07 habeas applications. The decision of a single judge to deny habeas applications, is consequently, outside limiting constitutional or statutory authority. There is no evidence that the Texas constitution or Statute suggests, 1. Texas Legislature intended to confer competent jurisdiction upon a single judge on the Ct. of Crim. App. to decide Art. 11.07 ' habeas applications. Whether he would be acting alone or as a proxy for a quorum of judges or 2. that C.C.A. 's decision-making responsibility is a delegable duty. Upon further review, Justice Keasler argues that "long-established practice" of this Court that permits 11.07 writ applications to be administratively and randomly assigned to a single judge for review and resolution, is a substitute for the provisions of Texas Constitution and Code. Justice Keasler's opinion. Id at 2. Considering these two opposing opinions, is the C.C.A. authorized to enact its own governing laws to delegate its competent jurisdiction upon a single judge, to act as a proxy for quorum of judges. Secondly, Because the Texas Legislature did not confer competent jurisdiction upon a single judge to decide habeas applications, is a decision rendered under such circumstances a void judgement?

  4. Is the Court of Criminal Appeals authorized to override Texas Rules of Appellate Procedure, Rule 76? Texas Rules of Appellate Procedure, Rule 76, Submission En Banc, states: "The Court will sit en banc to consider the following type of cases:(d) cases that were docketed under Code of Crim. Proc.., Article 11.07 or 11.071." The Legislative intent of enacting the above statute is clearly evident from its plain language. The statute requires the Ct. Crim. App. to convene en banc to consider the applications docketed under Code of Crim. Proc. Art. 11.07 or 11.071. The explicit text of the above statute further confirms Justice Alcala's assertion that "Texas Const. mandates that a quorum of judges decide this Court's non-capitol cases. This Court's internal administrative procedure that effectively operates as a standing order to permit a single budge to deny habeas relief for certain catagories of Art. 11.07 habeas applications based on a predetermined proxy vote in the absence of actual consideration of each case by a quorum fails to comply with the Texas Constitution and Code." Id at 7. It is especially noteworthy Justice Keasler in his response, conveniently refused to admit that provisions of Texas Rules of App. Pro., Rule 76(d) negates his argument. Because it requires the C.C.A. to convene enbanc when considering Art. 11.07 habeas applications. With this in mind, first, is the C.C.A. authorized to ignore the Legislative intent, and construct it's own internal administrative procedure to override Texas Rules of Appellate Procedure, Rule 76? And second, is the Court of Criminal Appeals internal administrative procedure constitutional?
