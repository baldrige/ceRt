William George Davis v. Texas

Question presented

CAPITAL CASE Texas defines capital murder as, inter alia, murdering “more than one person” during different criminal transactions but pursuant to the same scheme or course of conduct. Tex. Penal Code § 19.03(a)(7)(B). Petitioner was charged under this provision with murdering four hospital patients on four dates over seven months. Over Petitioner’s objection, the trial court instructed the jury that to convict him of capital murder, it had to agree unanimously that Petitioner had committed the “predicate offense” — the murder of the victim named first in the indictment — but that it “need not be unanimous” as to which, if any, of the other three named victims Petitioner had murdered, as long as all twelve jurors agreed that he had murdered at least one of them. The jury returned a general verdict finding Petitioner guilty of capital murder under § 19.03(a)(7)(B). The Question Presented is: May a State, consistent with the Sixth and Fourteenth Amendments, convict a defendant of capital murder for murdering “more than one person” in separate criminal transactions without requiring the jury to agree unanimously that he committed at least two specific acts of murder?
