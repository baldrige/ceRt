Question presented

The Federal Employers’ Liability Act, passed in 1908, creates a federal cause of action for railroad workers injured through employer negligence. 45 U.S.C. § 51. FELA’s negligence standard, drawn from the common law, requires “ordinary prudence.” Norfolk S. Ry. v. Sorrell, 549 U.S. 158, 169 (2007). What constitutes FELA negligence thus varies from case to case and jury to jury. In 1970, Congress—unsatisfied with the patchwork of existing laws related to railroad safety—passed the Federal Railroad Safety Act. The FRSA directs the Secretary of Transportation to issue regulations for “every area of railroad safety.” 49 U.S.C. § 20103(a). These regulations establish a uniform “Federal standard of care” for any “subject matter” they “cover[],” and state law is preempted from imposing “an additional or more stringent” standard. Id. § 20106(a)(2), (b)(1)(A). Thus, a railroad cannot be held negligent under state law for conduct that complied with on-point FRSA regulations. CSX Transp. v. Easterwood, 507 U.S. 658, 664–65, 675 (1993). The FRSA also mandates that all “[l]aws, regulations, and orders related to railroad safety . . . shall be nationally uniform to the extent practicable.” 49 U.S.C. § 20106(a)(1). The question presented, which has openly divided the lower courts, is: Whether FRSA regulations supply the standard of care for a FELA claim on the same subject, such that a FELA negligence claim is precluded to the same extent that a state negligence claim would be preempted.
