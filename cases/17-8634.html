Jason C. Youker v. United States

IFP petition · United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, No. 16-30135 · judgment December 7, 2017

Certiorari denied · May 29, 2018

Question presented

The Petitioner/Defendant was granted Pro Se status. Over his objection, the Court appointed Standby Counsel (hereinafter “SBC”) and, due to a representation by SBC that the investigator would not work directly with the Defendant, the Court placed SBC in charge of the Defendant’s access to the investigator working on the case. SBC failed and refused to convey the Defendant’s investigative requests to the investigator and later claimed it would violate his “ethics”. Not one of the Petitioner’s requests for investigation by contacting witnesses was honored. The District Court ignored the Defendant’s repeated efforts to have his Faretta rights honored, and never inquired about what “ethics” were involved. The Ninth Circuit also failed to honor the Petitioner’s Faretta and Constitutional rights when it affirmed the convictions. The questions presented are: 1- Whether a Court violates a Pro Se Defendant’s Faretta and Constitutional rights, including effective assistance of standby counsel, when it appoints standby counsel and places counsel in control of the incarcerated Pro Se Defendant’s access to an investigator, which resulted in a complete denial of all requests for a witness investigation by an investigator? 2- Did the Ninth Circuit and the District Court contravene United States Supreme Court precedent regarding the Defendant’s Fifth and Sixth Amendment Constitutional rights to represent himself when the orders and decisions entered herein allowed standby counsel to be in control of and overrule all of the Pro Se Defendant’s requests for investigation, in derogation of the Defendant’s right to make tactical decisions and control his case, thereby constituting structural error requiring dismissal or reversal?
