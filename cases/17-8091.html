Question presented

In Hazel-Atlas Co. v. Hartford-Empire Co., 322 U.S. 238 (1944), The Supreme Court held that in a case of after-discovered fraud, “relief will be granted against judgments regardless of the term of their entry.”’ The Court of Appeals had the power and the duty recall the ordered mandate. The Court of Appeals never acknowledged the failure of the attorneys to report taxable items concerning the deducted unemployment benefits the attorneys left unreported to the IRS in the motion to recall the mandate. The Second Circuit affirmed the District Court in it’s summary order based on the fact that the case was not a 301 Labor violation due to the reasoning that there was “no persuasive argument that Appellees were required to adopt Nelson’s preferred interpretation of ‘made whole’—the relevant language from the initial arbitrator award much less that the union ‘acted[ed] in such discriminatory, dishonest, arbitrary, or perfunctory fashion as to breach its duty to fair representation in failing to do so.” The Questions presented here are: Whether, under IRS code, an attorney has the duty to report all taxable items involved in a prIvate settlement. specifically, taxes required to be paid on unemployment benefits withheld from a union member as a result of the settlement.
