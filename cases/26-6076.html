Jeffrey I. Battle v. United States

IFP petition · United States Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit, No. 25-3723 · judgment August 3, 2026

Pending

Question presented

Petitioner Jeffrey I. Battle’s prison term was longer than it should have been because of a prior conviction for simple marijuana possession. He argued his low-level offense should have been excluded from his criminal history calculation under United States Sentencing Guidelines (“U.S.S.G.”) § 4A1.2(c). The text of that guideline asks whether his offense was “similar to” a list of exemplary crimes. Misdemeanor or petty offenses do not count as criminal history if the broadly stated similarity standard is met. Yet the United States Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit has examined whether prior marijuana-possession offenses count as criminal history based upon the commentary to the guidelines, which appeals to “common sense” and offers “relevant factors” that courts should consider, notwithstanding the guidelines’ text. Even accepting in past decisions that a majority of those relevant factors reveal that marijuana possession is similar to offenses excluded from one’s criminal history, the Sixth Circuit has consistently held that marijuana-possession offenses count, effectively applying a standard of strict equivalence, not similarity. As the Court considers whether deference to the commentary to the sentencing guidelines is owed, or even permitted, this case serves as an important companion. See Beaird v. United States, No. 25-5343. Battle asks the Court to consider: (1) is the “similar to” standard in § 4A1.2(c) met when offenses share characteristics, or only when they are the same in all relevant respects; and (2) is simple possession of marijuana “similar to” the other low-level mala prohibita offenses listed in § 4A1.2(c)(1) & (2)?
