Question presented

Virginia, like every other state, requires all motor vehicles to display a visible license plate with a unique letter and number combination that identifies the vehicle and to whom it is registered. Also like many other states, Virginia permits residents to choose their unique letter and number combination so long as they do not “carry a connotation that may be reasonably seen by a person viewing a license plate as 1) profane, obscene, or vulgar in nature, 2) sexually explicit or graphic, 3) excretory related, 4) describing intimate body parts or genitals, 5) describing drugs, drug culture or drug use, 6) condoning or encouraging violence, and/or 7) describing illegal activities or illegal substances.”1 The question presented is whether a state may, consistent with the First Amendment, limit individual expression on government-issued license plates, which are owned by the State and are issued by the State for the primary purpose of identifying motor vehicles and confirming registration with the state? 1 Va. Dep’t of Motor Vehicles, Form VSA 10, License Plate Application, at 2 (July 1, 2026), https://perma.cc/6VNG-B66B.
