Angela A. Garcia-Leija v. Flavio Faustino Leija

IFP petition · Court of Appeals of Washington, Division 2, No. 58824-2-II · judgment July 29, 2025

Pending

Questions presented

  1. Whether the Fourteenth Amendment's guarantee of parental liberty, as recognized in Troxel v. Granville, 530 U.S. 57 (2000), requires that a parent receive meaningful notice and a genuine opportunity to be heard before a court may impose a complete no-contact order severing all parent-child contact for an indefinite period - where the record affirmatively shows the parent was never lawfully served with the modification petition, appeared at the hearing and stated she had not received or read the moving papers, and the trial court itself acknowledged she needed a further opportunity to respond.

  2. Whether a state court violates the Fourteenth Amendment’s Due Process Clause by finding "waiver" of a constitutional objection - and affirming imposition of a court-access restriction barring a parent from filing any court document - where the finding of waiver rests on the parent's failure to file a written response to a motion she was never lawfully served with, and where the parent appeared at the hearing and objected to proceeding, in part, due to procedural defects.

  3. Whether a state may apply a domestic violence abusive litigation statute - enacted to protect domestic violence victims from abusive litigation by perpetrators (RCW 26.51.010) - against a petitioner who herself obtained a domestic violence protection order as a victim, thereby imposing a permanent court-access restriction that bars the victim from seeking judicial remedies, in violation of the First and Fourteenth Amendments.

  4. Whether the Due Process Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment permits adjudication of parental rights by a judge who (a) disclosed "huge bias" against the petitioner and a "lens so skewed over the duration of this case" in open court; (b) stated in October 2021 that the petitioner was "on your way to never seeing your kids again," then - when the petitioner confirmed that prediction on the record in September 2023 - responded "Well, good"; (c) pre­ coordinated with opposing counsel both the scheduling and the outcome of the modification proceeding that removed the petitioner’s children; and (d) imposed a permanent filing restriction against an unrepresented domestic violence victim - and where the appellate courts held this documented pattern did not constitute a "showing of bias" requiring reversal.

  5. Whether a state appellate court may invoke the mootness doctrine to avoid reviewing the ex parte, same-day quashing of a domestic violence protection order - on the ground that the temporary DVPO would have expired by its own terms - where (a) a temporary DVPO always expires by its terms and the court’s rule therefore categorically immunizes all DVPO quashings from appellate review, defeating the "capable of repetition yet evading review" exception; (b) the custody consequences of the quashing order persisted for years and were never moot; and (c) the state actor whose conduct is challenged caused the alleged mootness by quashing the order in the first place.
