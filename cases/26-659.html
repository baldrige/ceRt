Questions presented

Petitioner sought personal damages and prospective relief for a judge’s alleged property deprivations. The panel treated adjudicatory character as defeating jurisdiction and left immunity undecided. It also affirmed dismissal with prejudice of federal claims against two attorneys as duplicative, and affirmed dismissal of RICO claims against four nonbriefing defendants on pleading grounds not reached below while sustaining denial of amendment as futile.

  1. (a) Whether adjudicatory conduct alone defeats Article III adversity in a personal-capacity § 1983 damages suit for completed injuries, foreclosing the immunity exception for acts in the clear absence of all jurisdiction recognized in Stump v. Sparkman, 435 U.S. 349 (1978), and Mireles v. Waco, 502 U.S. 9 (1991). (b) Whether Whole Woman’s Health v. Jackson, 595 U.S. 30 (2021), makes adjudicatory character alone sufficient to defeat Article III adversity for official­ capacity prospective § 1983 relief, or requires examining the officer’s relation to the challenged conduct and requested relief.

  2. Whether a court may dismiss a second action with prejudice for claim splitting without comparing the transactions alleged in each action and determining whether concealment prevented their earlier assertion in the first; and whether any such dismissal must preserve prosecution in the first action against that dismissal’s own preclusive effect.

  3. Whether, under Singleton v. Wulff, 428 U.S. 106 (1976), briefing on one appellee’s objections affords a fair opportunity to address other defendants’ separately alleged conduct before appellate affirmance on pleading grounds not reached below and denial of amendment as futile.
