Friends of the Court

Who files amicus briefs, at which stage, on which side, beside whom — and who writes them — across OT2023–OT2025.

Terms: OT2023–OT2025All Terms

5,810 amicus briefs in 1,049 cases, from 2,776 filers other than the United States. 2,722 came in at the petition stage, asking the Court to take a case; 3,088 came after a grant, in 179 cases. As at the lectern, the field is long-tailed: 2,052 filers (74%) appear exactly once. Every floor on the page is scaled to the range. Over all 10 Terms a filer needs 8 decided cases to be rated and 10 merits briefs to be paired; over OT2023–OT2025 it needs 3 and 3.

Every brief here is classified by the same rules that colour the timeline on each case page, so the two always agree. The Counsel Table covers the parties’ own lawyers.

How to read this

Sides come from the calendar. The docket rarely says which side an amicus supports. Rule 37 does, by deadline: briefs supporting the petitioner or neither party are due a week after the petitioner’s merits brief, briefs supporting the respondent a week after the respondent’s. Where a brief’s file name happens to state its side, the deadline agrees on 97% of respondent-side briefs; about one in six on the petitioner’s schedule turns out to support neither party.

A filer is a name. Rows are keyed on the docket’s spelling with case, punctuation, a leading “The” and a corporate suffix set aside. Two different filers with the same name would merge; one filer that changes its name would split.

Briefs in paid and in forma pauperis cases belonging to OT2023–OT2025 — at the petition stage by the Term the case was docketed, on the merits by the Term it was argued — through October 7, 2026. The archive begins with the cases docketed in OT2017. Briefs on emergency applications and in original actions are not counted. A brief filed in consolidated cases counts once. Rejected filings, motions for leave and the Court-appointed amicus defending a judgment below are not amicus briefs here.
